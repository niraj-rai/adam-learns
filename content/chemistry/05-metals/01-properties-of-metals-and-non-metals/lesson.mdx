<ConceptCard title="The rule-breakers" emoji="🦄">
- **Mercury** is a metal but is **liquid** at room temperature (used in old thermometers).
- **Sodium** and **potassium** are metals soft enough to cut with a knife.
- **Graphite** (a form of carbon) is a non-metal that **conducts electricity**.
- **Iodine** is a non-metal that is **lustrous** (shiny).
- **Diamond** (also carbon) is the **hardest** natural substance, but it is a non-metal!
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="If it's shiny, it's a metal.">
Iodine crystals and diamonds are both shiny, but they're non-metals. Scientists never use just one property. They look at **several properties together**, just like a detective uses several clues.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Metals in an Indian kitchen">
- **Aluminium** pressure cookers: light and a good conductor of heat.
- **Copper**-bottomed pots: copper spreads heat evenly.
- **Stainless steel** thalis and katoris: strong and rust-free.
- **Brass** and **bronze** diyas and bells: they shine and ring beautifully.
</RealLife>
