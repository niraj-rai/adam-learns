<Hook emoji="💍">
At a jeweller's shop in Bengaluru, most necklaces are stamped **"22K 916"**, not 24K. That means they are only 91.6% gold. Is the jeweller cheating, or is there a good scientific reason?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Why is a little copper or silver added to gold for jewellery?"
  options={["To make it cheaper only", "Pure gold is too soft, and mixing makes it harder", "To make it shinier", "To make it magnetic"]}
  answer={1}
>
**Pure (24K) gold is very soft.** Fine chains would bend and scratch easily. Mixing in a little copper or silver makes an **alloy** that is much **harder** but still beautiful and rust-free. (It also changes the colour slightly: more copper gives a redder gold.)
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="What is an alloy?" />

<ConceptCard title="Alloys are mixtures" emoji="🥣">
An **alloy** is a **mixture** of a metal with one or more other elements (usually metals, sometimes carbon), made by melting them together.

Alloys are **mixtures, not compounds**: the proportions can vary, and each element keeps its identity. (Remember **brass** in Particle Zoom, Unit 2!)
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal>
<div>
**Why are alloys harder?** In a pure metal, all the atoms are the same size and sit in neat layers.
</div>
<div>
When you push hard, these layers can **slide** over each other easily. That's why pure metals are soft and malleable.
</div>
<div>
In an alloy, atoms of **different sizes** disrupt the neat layers, like a few big marbles mixed into small ones.
</div>
<div>
The layers **can't slide** as easily, so the alloy is **harder and stronger**.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<MythFact myth="An alloy is a compound.">
In a compound, elements are chemically **joined in a fixed ratio**. In an alloy, they're just **mixed**: brass can have anything from about 20% to 45% zinc. That makes alloys **mixtures**.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
The famous **Chola bronzes** of Tamil Nadu, like the dancing Nataraja, were cast over 1,000 years ago using the **lost-wax** method. Bronze (copper + tin) was perfect: strong, rust-free, and easy to cast into detailed shapes.
</DidYouKnow>
