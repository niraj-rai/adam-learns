<Hook emoji="🧴">
A plastic water bottle is used for about **10 minutes**. It could stay in the environment for around **450 years**. What makes plastics so useful, and such a big problem?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Plastics, nylon and polyester are all made of…"
  options={["Tiny metal crystals", "Very long chains of small repeating units (polymers)", "Pure carbon atoms", "Sand"]}
  answer={1}
>
They are **polymers**: giant molecules made by joining thousands of small units (**monomers**) into long chains, like beads on a necklace. Most are made from chemicals obtained from **petroleum**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Polymers all around you" />

<ConceptCard title="Synthetic fibres" emoji="🧵">
- **Nylon**: very strong. Used in ropes, toothbrush bristles, parachutes and fishing lines.
- **Polyester**: doesn't wrinkle easily. Used in shirts and sarees (often blended with cotton as "polycot").
- **Acrylic**: wool-like and cheap. Used in sweaters and blankets.
- **Rayon**: made from wood pulp, but it looks and feels like silk.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Why do we love plastics?" emoji="👍">
They are **light**, **strong**, **cheap**, **easy to mould**, **don't rust**, are **poor conductors** of heat and electricity, and **don't react** with most chemicals.

That last property is also the problem: they **don't break down** in nature.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="How long does it last?" />

<Lab id="plastic-timeline" />

<MythFact myth="Plastic eventually disappears.">
Most plastic doesn't truly disappear. Sunlight and waves break it into smaller and smaller pieces called **microplastics**, which have been found in the ocean, in fish, in salt, and even in human blood. The chains are still there, just tiny.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="India's fight against plastic">
India banned many **single-use plastic** items (like plastic cutlery, straws and thin carry bags) from **July 2022**. Karnataka had its own ban from 2016. Many Bengaluru shops now ask you to bring a cloth bag, and some cities build roads using shredded plastic waste mixed with bitumen!
</RealLife>
