<Hook emoji="🧼">
Oil and water don't mix, so water alone can't wash an oily stain. Soap molecules have a split personality: one end loves water, the other loves oil. Together they lift grease away.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Why is soap less effective in hard water?"

  answer={1}
>
Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions form an insoluble **scum** with soap, wasting it.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Key ideas" />

<ConceptCard title="Heads and tails" emoji="🧼">
A soap is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain fatty acid. Its ionic **head** is <KeyTerm def="Water-loving: attracted to water.">hydrophilic</KeyTerm>; its long hydrocarbon **tail** is <KeyTerm def="Water-hating: repelled by water but attracted to oils.">hydrophobic</KeyTerm>. In water, tails bury into grease and heads face out, forming <KeyTerm def="A cluster of soap molecules with tails pointing inwards around oil and heads outwards in the water.">micelles</KeyTerm> that rinse away.
</ConceptCard>
