<Hook emoji="🤝">
Carbon has 4 outer electrons. Losing or gaining 4 would take far too much energy, so carbon does something different: it **shares**. That one habit makes diamonds, pencil lead and every molecule in your body possible.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="How many electron pairs are shared between the two nitrogen atoms in N₂?"
  options={["1", "2", "3", "4"]}
  answer={2}
>
Each N needs 3 more electrons, so they share **three pairs**: a triple bond.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Key ideas" />

<ConceptCard title="Covalent bonds" emoji="🤝">
A <KeyTerm def="A bond formed when two atoms share a pair of electrons.">covalent bond</KeyTerm> is a shared pair of electrons. Sharing two pairs makes a double bond (O=O), three a triple bond (N≡N). Covalent compounds are usually molecules with weak forces between them, so they have **low melting and boiling points** and **don't conduct**.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Allotropes of carbon" emoji="💎">
<KeyTerm def="Different structural forms of the same element, like diamond and graphite for carbon.">Allotropes</KeyTerm>: diamond (each C bonded to 4 others: hardest), graphite (layers, each C bonded to 3: soft, conducts) and fullerenes (C₆₀ cages). Carbon also shows <KeyTerm def="The ability of an element to form bonds with atoms of the same element, making long chains and rings.">catenation</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>
