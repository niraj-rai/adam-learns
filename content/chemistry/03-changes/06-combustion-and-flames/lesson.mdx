<Stage kind="wonder" title="Why won't the paper cup burn?" />

<Hook emoji="🥤">
Fill a paper cup with water and hold it over a candle flame. The cup doesn't catch fire, and the water gets warm! But an empty paper cup bursts into flames in seconds. What is the water doing?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Why doesn't the paper cup with water burn?"
  options={["Water is fireproof", "The water absorbs the heat, keeping the paper below the temperature at which it catches fire", "There is no oxygen near the cup", "Paper cannot burn"]}
  answer={1}
>
The water absorbs the heat, so the paper never gets hot enough to catch fire. Every substance has a minimum temperature it must reach before it burns: its **ignition temperature**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="What is combustion?" />

<ConceptCard title="Combustion = burning" emoji="🔥">
**Combustion** is a chemical reaction in which a substance reacts with **oxygen**, giving out **heat** (and often **light**). The substance that burns is called a **fuel**.

When fuels containing carbon and hydrogen (wood, LPG, petrol, wax) burn completely:
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="\text{fuel} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} + \text{energy}" />

<ConceptCard title="The fire triangle" emoji="🔺">
A fire needs **three** things at the same time:

1. **Fuel**: something to burn
2. **Oxygen**: usually from the air
3. **Heat**: enough to reach the ignition temperature

Remove **any one side** and the fire goes out. Every fire extinguisher works by taking away one (or more) sides.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Inflammable substances" emoji="⚠️">
Substances with a very **low ignition temperature**, like petrol, LPG, alcohol and kerosene, catch fire very easily. They are called **inflammable** (or flammable) and must be stored carefully, away from flames and sparks.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="The yellow, bright part of a flame is the hottest.">
The **pale blue outer zone** is the hottest, because combustion is complete there. The yellow part glows because of tiny hot **soot particles** that haven't burned completely. That's why goldsmiths use the outermost zone.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
**Carbon monoxide** from incomplete combustion is colourless and has no smell, so you can't tell it's there. Every winter, people are harmed by burning coal or charcoal heaters in closed rooms. Always keep a room ventilated when anything is burning!
</DidYouKnow>
