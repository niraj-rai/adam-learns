<Stage kind="explain" title="Five signs to look for" />

<ConceptCard title="Signs that a new substance may have formed" emoji="🔎">
1. **Gas is given off:** fizzing or bubbles (*effervescence*), or a new smell.
2. **Colour change:** a colour appears, disappears or changes.
3. **Temperature change:** it gets hotter or colder.
4. **A solid forms** when two solutions are mixed: a *precipitate*, often making the liquid cloudy or milky.
5. **Light or sound:** a glow, a flash, a pop.

One sign alone isn't proof: boiling water bubbles too! But several signs together are strong evidence of a chemical reaction.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="The lime-water test for carbon dioxide" emoji="🥛">
**Lime water** is a clear solution of calcium hydroxide. Bubble **carbon dioxide** through it and it turns **milky**, because a white solid (calcium carbonate) forms.

This is the standard test chemists use to identify carbon dioxide. Try breathing out through a straw into lime water in the lab above: your breath contains carbon dioxide!
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Bubbles always mean a chemical reaction.">
Boiling water bubbles, and so does a fizzy drink when you open it. That's dissolved carbon dioxide escaping. Neither makes a new substance. Look for **other evidence** too, or **test the gas**.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Making paneer">
Add lemon juice to hot milk and it separates into white lumps and a watery liquid. The acid makes milk proteins clump together: a solid forms from a liquid. Strain the lumps through a cloth and you have **paneer**!
</RealLife>

<Stage kind="apply" title="Plan an investigation" />

<Inquiry criterion="B" title="Which gives the biggest fizz?">
**Research question:** *Does the amount of baking soda affect how much gas is produced with vinegar?*

1. Identify your **independent**, **dependent** and **controlled** variables.
2. How could you **measure** the amount of gas? (Hint: stretch a balloon over the bottle's mouth, and measure its size.)
3. Write a **hypothesis** with a reason.
4. What safety precautions are needed?
</Inquiry>

<TryAtHome
  title="Balloon inflator"
  materials={["an empty plastic bottle", "a balloon", "vinegar", "baking soda", "a funnel or folded paper"]}
  safety="Do this over a sink or tray. Don't point the bottle at anyone's face."
>
1. Pour about 3 cm of vinegar into the bottle.
2. Use a funnel to put 2 teaspoons of baking soda into the balloon.
3. Stretch the balloon over the bottle's neck, then lift it so the soda falls in.
4. Watch the balloon inflate with carbon dioxide! Touch the bottle: is it warmer or colder?
</TryAtHome>
