<Stage kind="explain" title="Symbols and formulas" />

<ConceptCard title="Element symbols" emoji="🔤">
Every element has a **symbol** of one or two letters. The **first letter is always a capital**, and the second is always small.

- From English names: H (hydrogen), C (carbon), O (oxygen), N (nitrogen), Cl (chlorine), Mg (magnesium)
- From Latin names: **Na** (sodium, *natrium*), **Fe** (iron, *ferrum*), **Cu** (copper, *cuprum*), **Ag** (silver, *argentum*), **Au** (gold, *aurum*), **K** (potassium, *kalium*)

Capitals matter! **Co** is cobalt (an element), but **CO** is carbon monoxide (a compound of C and O).
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Reading a formula" emoji="🔍">
A **formula** shows which elements are in a substance and how many atoms of each.

The small number written **after and below** a symbol (a *subscript*) counts only that atom:

- <Chem>H2O</Chem>: 2 hydrogen + 1 oxygen = 3 atoms
- <Chem>CH4</Chem> (methane): 1 carbon + 4 hydrogen = 5 atoms
- <Chem>CaCO3</Chem> (chalk): 1 calcium + 1 carbon + 3 oxygen = 5 atoms
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Word equations" />

<ConceptCard title="Reactants → products" emoji="➡️">
A **word equation** describes a reaction in words.

- **Reactants** are the substances you start with (on the **left**).
- **Products** are the new substances formed (on the **right**).
- The **arrow** means "react to form" or "turn into". It does **not** mean "equals".
- A **+** sign separates two or more reactants or products.
</ConceptCard>

<div>

</div>

</div>

</div>

<MythFact myth="In CO₂, the 2 means there are two of everything.">
A subscript counts **only the atom just before it**. <Chem>CO2</Chem> has **one** carbon and **two** oxygens. To show two whole molecules we write a big number in front: <Chem>2CO2</Chem> means 2 molecules = 2 carbons and 4 oxygens.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
The modern symbols were introduced by the Swedish chemist **Jöns Jakob Berzelius** in 1813. Before that, chemists (and alchemists) used strange pictures, like a circle with a dot for gold!
</DidYouKnow>
