<Stage kind="wonder" title="Where did the log go?" />

<Hook emoji="🔥">
A 10 kg log burns in a campfire. In the morning, only a small heap of ash is left. It weighs less than 1 kg. Where did the other 9 kg go? Did the mass just vanish?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Steel wool is burned on a balance. What happens to the reading?"
  options={["It goes down, as with the log", "It goes up", "It stays exactly the same", "It drops to zero"]}
  answer={1}
>
Surprise: the mass goes **up**! The iron **joins with oxygen** from the air to make iron oxide, and that oxygen has mass. With the log, the opposite happens: most of the wood turns into gases (carbon dioxide and water vapour) that **escape into the air**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="The law of conservation of mass" />

<ConceptCard title="Mass is never created or destroyed" emoji="⚖️">
In a chemical reaction, **atoms are rearranged, never created or destroyed**. So:

> **Total mass of reactants = total mass of products**

This is the **law of conservation of mass**, stated by the French chemist **Antoine Lavoisier** in 1789, who carefully weighed reactions in **sealed** containers.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Using the law in calculations" emoji="🧮">
If 24 g of magnesium burns completely in 16 g of oxygen, how much magnesium oxide forms?

Mass of products = mass of reactants = 24 g + 16 g = **40 g** of magnesium oxide.
</ConceptCard>

<QuickCheck question="12 g of carbon burns to make 44 g of carbon dioxide. What mass of oxygen was used?" options={["12 g", "32 g", "44 g", "56 g"]} answer={1}>
Reactants = products, so 12 g + oxygen = 44 g. Oxygen = 44 − 12 = **32 g**.
</QuickCheck>

<MythFact myth="Gases don't weigh anything.">
Gases are made of particles, and particles have mass. Remember that the air in your classroom weighs about 200 kg (Topic 1.1)! When gas escapes from an open flask, the balance reading drops because real mass has left.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Lavoisier is called the **"father of modern chemistry"**. Before him, many scientists believed burning released a mysterious substance called *phlogiston*. By weighing carefully, he showed that burning is actually a reaction with **oxygen**, a gas he named.
</DidYouKnow>
