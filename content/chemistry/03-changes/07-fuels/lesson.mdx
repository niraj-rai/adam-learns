<Hook emoji="🦕">
The petrol in a scooter and the coal burned in a power station hold energy from **sunlight that fell on Earth millions of years ago**. How did ancient sunshine end up in a fuel tank?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Fossil fuels like coal and petroleum mainly formed from…"
  options={["Dinosaur bones", "Dead plants and tiny sea organisms buried for millions of years", "Volcanic lava", "Rocks from space"]}
  answer={1}
>
**Coal** formed from ancient forests, and **petroleum and natural gas** from tiny sea plants and animals (plankton). Buried under mud and rock, they were slowly changed by **heat and pressure** over millions of years. Plants captured the energy from sunlight, and it stayed locked inside.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Fossil fuels" />

<ConceptCard title="Non-renewable" emoji="⏳">
Fossil fuels took **millions of years** to form, but we are using them up in a few hundred years. Once used, they cannot be replaced in a human lifetime, so they are **non-renewable**.

India mines coal in Jharkhand, Odisha and Chhattisgarh, and extracts petroleum at places like Mumbai High (offshore) and in Assam. The first oil well in Asia was drilled at **Digboi, Assam**, in 1889.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Refining crude oil" emoji="🏭">
**Crude oil** (petroleum) is a thick, dark mixture of many substances. At a **refinery**, it is separated by **fractional distillation** (remember Topic 2.5?):

The oil is heated until it turns to vapour, which rises up a tall column that is **hot at the bottom and cooler at the top**. Each part (**fraction**) condenses at a different height depending on its boiling point.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="What makes a good fuel?" emoji="✅">
- High **calorific value** (lots of energy per gram)
- Burns **easily** but not dangerously
- Leaves little **ash** or smoke, and gives few **harmful gases**
- **Cheap**, easy to **store** and **transport**, and readily available

No fuel is perfect. That's why we still use so many different ones!
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="\text{calorific value} = \frac{\text{heat energy released}}{\text{mass of fuel burned}} \quad (\text{kJ/kg or kJ/g})" />

<MythFact myth="The fuel with the highest calorific value is always best.">
**Hydrogen** has the highest calorific value (about 150 kJ/g) and only makes water when it burns. But it is hard to store, easily explodes, and today is mostly made using fossil fuels. The "best" fuel depends on **cost, safety, storage and pollution** too.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Cleaner fuels on Indian roads">
After the Supreme Court ordered Delhi's buses and autos to switch to **CNG** in the early 2000s, the city's air improved noticeably. CNG burns more completely than diesel, producing far less soot. Today, **electric** scooters and buses are the next big shift.
</RealLife>
