<Stage kind="wonder" title="The wrong water" />

<Hook emoji="🧯">
Water is the most common way to put out fires. But throw water on a kadhai of burning oil and a giant fireball can erupt. Why can the thing that saves lives in one fire be dangerous in another?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Why is water dangerous on a burning oil or petrol fire?"
  options={["Water catches fire", "Oil floats on water, and water flash-boils under hot oil, spreading the burning oil", "Water adds oxygen", "It isn't dangerous at all"]}
  answer={1}
>
Oil and petrol are **less dense** than water and **float** on it, so the burning liquid spreads. With very hot oil, water sinks, instantly **boils into steam**, and blasts burning oil into the air. You need to cut off **oxygen** instead.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Match the extinguisher to the fire" />

| Type of fire | What to use | Never use |
|---|---|---|
| Paper, wood, cloth | Water, sand | (water is fine here) |
| Cooking oil | Turn off heat; lid, damp cloth or fire blanket | Water |
| Petrol, kerosene | Sand, foam or CO₂ extinguisher | Water |
| Electrical | Switch off power; CO₂ or dry powder extinguisher | Water |

<ConceptCard title="How a CO₂ extinguisher works" emoji="🧯">
Carbon dioxide is **heavier than air** and does not support burning. Sprayed from the extinguisher, it forms a blanket around the fire that **cuts off oxygen**. It also **cools** the fuel, because it comes out very cold as it expands. It leaves no mess and doesn't conduct electricity, so it is ideal for electrical equipment.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="In an emergency in India">
- **101**: Fire brigade · **112**: all emergencies
- **Get out first, then call.** Never go back in for belongings.
- In smoke, **crawl low**: cleaner air is near the floor.
- For an LPG leak, call your **gas agency's emergency number** (printed on the cylinder booklet).
</RealLife>
