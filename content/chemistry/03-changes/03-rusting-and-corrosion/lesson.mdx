<Stage kind="explain" title="What is rust?" />

<ConceptCard title="Rusting is a chemical change" emoji="🟫">
When iron is exposed to **oxygen** (from air) and **water** (or moisture), it slowly reacts to form **rust**, a reddish-brown, flaky substance called hydrated **iron oxide**.

<Formula tex="\text{iron} + \text{oxygen} + \text{water} \rightarrow \text{rust (hydrated iron oxide)}" />

Rust is a **new substance** with different properties: it is brittle, flakes off and is not magnetic like iron. As it flakes off, fresh iron underneath is exposed and rusts too, so iron can rust right through.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Corrosion" emoji="🪙">
**Corrosion** is the general name for metals being slowly eaten away by substances around them. **Rusting** is the corrosion of **iron**.

Other metals corrode too: silver turns black (tarnishes) and copper turns green, like the Statue of Liberty!
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal>
<div>
**Preventing rusting: keep oxygen and water away.**
</div>
<div>
🎨 **Painting** gates, cars and bridges puts a barrier between the iron and the air and water.
</div>
<div>
🛢️ **Oiling or greasing** moving parts, like a bicycle chain, where paint would rub off.
</div>
<div>
🧱 **Galvanising:** coating iron with a layer of **zinc**. Even if scratched, the zinc corrodes first and protects the iron. That's why corrugated roof sheets and water pipes are galvanised.
</div>
<div>
🍴 **Making alloys:** **stainless steel** (iron mixed with chromium and nickel) doesn't rust. That's why kitchen utensils and spoons are made from it.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<MythFact myth="Rust is just dirt stuck on the iron.">
You can wipe off dirt and find shiny iron underneath. Rust is the iron itself, **changed into a new substance**. Once iron has rusted, that part of the metal is gone.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Scientists at IIT Kanpur found that Delhi's Iron Pillar is protected by a thin, tough layer called **misawite** that formed because the ancient iron contained lots of **phosphorus**. The layer stops oxygen and water from reaching the iron inside. Ancient Indian metallurgy was ahead of its time!
</DidYouKnow>
