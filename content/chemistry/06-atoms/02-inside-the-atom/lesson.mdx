<Hook emoji="🔢">
Gold and lead are both heavy grey-yellow metals. Alchemists spent centuries trying to turn lead into gold. The difference is just **3 tiny particles** in each atom's nucleus. What are they?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst

  options={["The number of neutrons", "The number of electrons", "The number of protons", "The size of the atom"]}
  answer={2}
>
The number of **protons** is the atom's identity card. Every atom with 6 protons is carbon; every atom with 79 is gold. Lead has 82, just 3 more than gold!
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Three particles" />

| Particle | Charge | Relative mass | Where it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | +1 | 1 | in the nucleus |
| **Neutron** | 0 | 1 | in the nucleus |
| **Electron** | −1 | about 1/1840 (almost zero) | in shells around the nucleus |

<ConceptCard title="Two important numbers" emoji="🏷️">
- **Atomic number (Z)** = number of **protons**. It decides the element.
- **Mass number (A)** = **protons + neutrons** (electrons are too light to count).

So: **neutrons = mass number − atomic number**.

In a neutral atom, **electrons = protons**, so the charges cancel out.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Ions: atoms that gained or lost electrons" emoji="⚡">
Atoms can **lose or gain electrons** (never protons in chemistry!):

- **Lose** electrons → more protons than electrons → a **positive ion** (cation), e.g. Na⁺
- **Gain** electrons → more electrons than protons → a **negative ion** (anion), e.g. Cl⁻

Table salt is made of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions held together, the lattice you saw in Particle Zoom!
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Adding an electron turns an atom into a different element.">
Only changing the number of **protons** changes the element. Add an electron to chlorine and you still have chlorine, just as a **chloride ion** (Cl⁻). Add a neutron and it's still chlorine, just a heavier **isotope** (next topic).
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Protons and neutrons are made of even smaller particles called **quarks**. Satyendra Nath Bose, an Indian physicist, worked out rules for a family of particles now called **bosons**, named after him!
</DidYouKnow>
