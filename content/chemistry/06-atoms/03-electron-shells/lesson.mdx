<Hook emoji="🎈">
Helium in a balloon never burns or reacts. Sodium explodes in water. Fluorine attacks almost anything. The secret is **how the electrons are arranged** around the nucleus, especially the ones on the outside.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="The first electron shell can hold 2 electrons. How many can the second shell hold?"
  options={["2", "4", "8", "Unlimited"]}
  answer={2}
>
The second shell can hold up to **8** electrons. For the first 20 elements, the rule is **2, then 8, then 8** (and then 2 more in the fourth shell for potassium and calcium).
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Filling the shells" />

<ConceptCard title="The 2, 8, 8 rule" emoji="🎯">
Electrons fill the shell **closest to the nucleus first**, then move outwards:

- Shell 1: up to **2** electrons
- Shell 2: up to **8** electrons
- Shell 3: up to **8** electrons (for the first 20 elements)

Examples: carbon (6 electrons) = **2, 4**. Sodium (11) = **2, 8, 1**. Chlorine (17) = **2, 8, 7**. Argon (18) = **2, 8, 8**.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Valence electrons and valency" emoji="🤝">
The electrons in the **outermost shell** are the **valence electrons**. They take part in reactions.

Atoms are most stable with a **full outer shell** (2 for the first shell, 8 for others). That's why noble gases don't react.

**Valency** is the number of electrons an atom must **lose, gain or share** to get a full outer shell:

- Sodium 2, 8, **1**: lose 1 → valency **1**
- Magnesium 2, 8, **2**: lose 2 → valency **2**
- Oxygen 2, **6**: gain 2 → valency **2**
- Chlorine 2, 8, **7**: gain 1 → valency **1**
- Carbon 2, **4**: share 4 → valency **4**
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Valency always equals the number of outer electrons.">
That works for 1–4 outer electrons. But chlorine has 7 outer electrons and a valency of **1**. It's easier to **gain 1** than to lose 7! For 5–7 outer electrons, valency = **8 − outer electrons**.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
The colours of Diwali fireworks come from electrons! When metal atoms are heated, electrons jump to higher shells and then fall back, giving out light of a particular colour: sodium gives yellow, strontium red, barium green and copper blue-green.
</DidYouKnow>
