<Hook emoji="🏺">
Archaeologists dig up a piece of ancient wood and can tell it's about 3,000 years old. There's no date written on it. The clue is hidden inside some of its **carbon atoms**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are both carbon. What is different about them?"
  options={["The number of protons", "The number of neutrons", "The number of electrons", "Nothing at all"]}
  answer={1}
>
They have the **same number of protons** (6), so both are carbon, but a different number of **neutrons**: 6 in carbon-12 and 8 in carbon-14. Atoms like this are called **isotopes**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Same element, different mass" />

<ConceptCard title="Isotopes" emoji="⚖️">
**Isotopes** are atoms of the **same element** (same number of protons) with **different numbers of neutrons**, and so different mass numbers.

- Hydrogen-1 (no neutrons), deuterium H-2 (1 neutron), tritium H-3 (2 neutrons)
- Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37
- Carbon-12, carbon-13 and carbon-14

Isotopes have the **same chemical properties** (same electrons), but slightly different **physical** properties like mass and density.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Relative atomic mass" emoji="🧮">
Natural chlorine is about **75% chlorine-35** and **25% chlorine-37**. That's why the periodic table gives chlorine's atomic mass as **35.5**, not a whole number: it's an **average** weighted by how common each isotope is.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="(35 \times 75 + 37 \times 25) \div 100 = 35.5" caption="Weighted average mass of chlorine atoms" />

<MythFact myth="Isotopes are always radioactive and dangerous.">
Most isotopes are perfectly **stable**: carbon-12 and carbon-13 in your body, chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 in salt. Only some, like carbon-14 and uranium-235, are **radioactive**. Even radioactive isotopes can be used safely by experts.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Isotopes at work in India">
- **Medicine:** radioactive iodine-131 treats thyroid problems; cobalt-60 is used in cancer radiotherapy (e.g. at Tata Memorial Hospital, Mumbai).
- **Energy:** uranium-235 fuels India's nuclear power stations, such as Kudankulam in Tamil Nadu.
- **Archaeology:** carbon-14 dating tells us the age of ancient wood, bones and cloth.
- **Agriculture:** BARC (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre) uses radiation to develop improved crop varieties.
</RealLife>
