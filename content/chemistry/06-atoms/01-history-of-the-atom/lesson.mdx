An atom is so small that **millions** of them would fit side by side across the full stop at the end of this sentence. No one could see one until the late 20th century. So how did scientists work out what's inside?
</Hook>

<Stage kind="explain" title="A 2,500-year story" />

<StepReveal>
<div>
**Over 2,000 years ago: Maharishi Kanad** (India) suggests matter is made of tiny, indivisible particles called **paramanu**. Around the same time, the Greek thinker **Democritus** called them *atomos*, meaning "uncuttable". These were ideas, not yet tested by experiments.
</div>
<div>
**1808: John Dalton** (England) turns the idea into science: all matter is made of **atoms**, atoms of one element are identical, and atoms combine in fixed ratios to make compounds. He imagined atoms as tiny **solid spheres**.
</div>
<div>
**1897: J.J. Thomson** discovers the **electron**, a tiny negative particle inside atoms. So atoms *can* be divided! He proposes the **plum pudding model**: negative electrons dotted through a ball of positive charge.
</div>
<div>
**1909–1911: Ernest Rutherford** and his team fire alpha particles at gold foil. A few bounce back, so the positive charge must be packed into a tiny, dense centre: the **nucleus**. The rest of the atom is mostly empty space.
</div>
<div>
**1913: Niels Bohr** suggests that electrons move around the nucleus in fixed **shells** (energy levels). This explains why elements give out particular colours of light.
</div>
<div>
**1932: James Chadwick** discovers the **neutron**, a neutral particle in the nucleus. Now we have the three particles you'll build with in the next topic!
</div>
</StepReveal>

<Stage kind="try" title="Repeat Rutherford's experiment" />

<Lab id="gold-foil" />

<ConceptCard title="What the gold foil results tell us" emoji="🧠">
- **Most alpha particles went straight through** → the atom is mostly **empty space**.
- **Some were deflected** → there is something **positive** inside that repels the positive alpha particles.
- **Very few bounced back** → that positive part is **tiny** and very **dense**: the **nucleus**.

If an atom were the size of a cricket stadium, the nucleus would be about the size of a **pea** in the middle!
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="The scientists with the old models were wrong, so they were bad scientists.">
Each model **explained the evidence available at the time**. Thomson's model was excellent until Rutherford's experiment produced results it couldn't explain. Science improves by **testing models and replacing them** when better evidence appears. Today's model will probably be improved one day too!
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Rutherford's gold foil experiments were actually done by his young assistants, **Hans Geiger** and **Ernest Marsden**, who spent hours in dark rooms counting tiny flashes of light made by alpha particles on a screen.
</DidYouKnow>
