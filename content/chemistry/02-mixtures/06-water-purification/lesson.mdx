<PredictFirst

>
Clear water can still carry **germs** (bacteria, viruses) that cause diseases like cholera and typhoid, and **dissolved** harmful substances. You can't see either. Safe drinking water is called **potable** water.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="The four stages of water treatment" />

<StepReveal>
<div>
**1. Screening:** water passes through **screens** (meshes) that catch floating leaves, sticks, plastic and fish. The biggest things come out first.
</div>
<div>
**2. Loading & sedimentation:** **alum** (phitkari) is added. It makes tiny clay particles stick together into heavier clumps (*loading*). The clumps **settle** in big tanks, and the clearer water is drawn off the top (*decantation*).
</div>
<div>
**3. Filtration:** the water trickles through beds of **sand and gravel**, which trap the fine particles still floating in it.
</div>
<div>
**4. Disinfection:** **chlorine** is added to kill germs. Some cities also use ozone or ultraviolet light. At home, **boiling** water for a few minutes also kills germs.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<ConceptCard title="Why this order?" emoji="🔢">
Always remove the **biggest** things first and the **smallest** things (germs) last:

- Big debris would **clog** every later stage.
- Mud would **block** sand filters quickly.
- Germs can **hide inside** dirt particles, where chlorine can't reach them, so disinfection works best on clear water.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="A filter cleans water completely.">
Ordinary filters remove **solid particles**. Most germs are small enough to get through a sand filter, and **dissolved** substances like salts and some harmful chemicals pass straight through. That's why disinfection is always the last stage, and why some home purifiers use **reverse osmosis (RO)** or UV light as well.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Water at home in India">
Many Indian homes add one more stage at the tap: a **candle filter**, an **RO purifier**, or a **UV purifier**. RO purifiers remove dissolved salts too, but they waste a lot of water: often 2–3 litres for every litre of drinking water. Some RO waste water can be reused for mopping or watering plants.
</RealLife>

<DidYouKnow>
The WHO estimates that unsafe drinking water still causes **hundreds of thousands of deaths** every year worldwide, mostly of young children. India's **Jal Jeevan Mission** aims to give every rural home a working tap with safe drinking water.
</DidYouKnow>
