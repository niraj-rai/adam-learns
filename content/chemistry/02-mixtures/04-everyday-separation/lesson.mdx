<PredictFirst
  question="A mixture of sand and salt. Which PROPERTY could help you separate them?"
  options={["Colour", "Salt dissolves in water but sand doesn't", "They have different smells", "Sand is magnetic"]}
  answer={1}
>
The trick is **solubility**. Add water: the salt dissolves but the sand doesn't. Filter out the sand, then evaporate the water to get the salt back. Every separation method works by using a **property that is different**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Find the difference, pick the method" />

| Method | Property used | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hand-picking** | Size, colour or shape (easy to see) | Stones out of rice or dal |
| **Threshing** | Grains attached to stalks | Beating wheat stalks to free the grains |
| **Winnowing** | Weight: light parts blow away | Separating husk from grain in the wind |
| **Sieving** | Particle size | Removing bran from atta; tea strainer |
| **Magnetic separation** | Magnetism | Pulling iron pieces out of scrap |
| **Churning** | Density: lighter fat separates | Butter from curd or buttermilk |
| **Sedimentation & decantation** | Heavy insoluble solid settles | Letting muddy water stand, then pouring off |
| **Filtration** | Insoluble solid vs liquid | Filter paper, cloth, water filters |
| **Evaporation** | Dissolved solid vs liquid | Salt from sea water in salt pans |

<ConceptCard title="Filtration words" emoji="🧻">
When you filter, the solid left on the filter paper is the <KeyTerm def="The insoluble solid trapped by the filter paper.">residue</KeyTerm>. The clear liquid that passes through is the <KeyTerm def="The liquid that passes through the filter paper.">filtrate</KeyTerm>.

Filter paper has tiny holes. Water particles and **dissolved** particles pass straight through, but bigger insoluble grains get stuck.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="You can filter the salt out of salt water.">
Dissolved salt particles are so tiny they slip through filter paper along with the water. The filtrate is **still salty**. To get dissolved salt back, you need **evaporation** (or distillation, next topic).
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Salt pans of Gujarat and Tamil Nadu">
About **three-quarters of India's salt** comes from Gujarat. Sea water is let into shallow, flat pans, and the sun and wind **evaporate** the water over many weeks, leaving salt crystals that workers rake into white mountains. It's evaporation on a gigantic scale!
</RealLife>

- NCERT examples to know: threshing, winnowing, hand-picking, sieving, **churning** (butter from curd), sedimentation, decantation, filtration and evaporation.
- NCERT also uses **loading**: adding alum (phitkari) to muddy water makes the mud settle faster. You'll use it in the Boss Challenge!
