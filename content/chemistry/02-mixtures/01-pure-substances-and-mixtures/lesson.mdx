Milk is made of **many different substances** mixed together: mostly water, plus fat, proteins (like casein), a sugar called lactose, and minerals. To a scientist, that makes it a **mixture**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Pure means ONE kind of particle" />

<ConceptCard title="Pure substance" emoji="💎">
In science, a **pure substance** is made of **only one kind of particle**. Every particle is exactly the same.

- Distilled water: only water molecules.
- Gold: only gold atoms.
- Table salt: only sodium chloride.

On a food label, "pure" usually means *nothing else has been added* (no adulteration). That's useful, but it's **not** the scientific meaning.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Mixture" emoji="🥗">
A **mixture** contains **two or more different substances** that are physically mixed but **not chemically joined**.

- Each substance keeps its **own properties**. Salt water still tastes salty!
- The amounts can **vary**. You can make weak or strong salt water.
- Mixtures can be separated by **physical methods** such as filtering or evaporating.
</ConceptCard>

<QuickCheck question="Which is a HOMOGENEOUS mixture?" options={["Oil and water", "Sugar dissolved in water", "Sand and iron filings", "Poha with peanuts"]} answer={1}>
Sugar water looks and tastes **the same everywhere** in the glass, and you can't see the sugar. It's homogeneous. The others have parts you can see.
</QuickCheck>

<ConceptCard title="How can you TEST if something is pure?" emoji="🌡️">
A pure substance **melts and boils at one exact temperature**. Pure water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C (at sea level).

A mixture melts or boils **over a range** of temperatures, and impurities change the values. Salt water boils **above** 100 °C and freezes **below** 0 °C.

That's why chemists measure melting points to check purity, and why salt is sprinkled on icy roads in cold countries!
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="If it looks the same all over, it must be pure.">
**Salt water** looks exactly like pure water. **Air** looks like nothing at all. **Brass** looks like a single shiny metal. All three are **mixtures**! Homogeneous mixtures hide their parts so well that you need tests (like boiling point) or the particle model to tell.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Most of the matter around you is a mixture. Even the **tap water** in your home is a mixture: it has tiny amounts of dissolved minerals and a little chlorine. Truly pure water is made in labs by distillation, and it actually tastes quite flat!
</DidYouKnow>
