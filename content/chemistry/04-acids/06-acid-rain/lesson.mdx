<Hook emoji="🕌">
The **Taj Mahal** has stood for nearly 400 years, but in recent decades its white marble has been turning yellowish and pitted. Scientists and even India's Supreme Court got involved. What was attacking the marble?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Marble is calcium carbonate. What happens when acid rain falls on it?"
  options={["Nothing, since marble is too hard", "The acid reacts with the carbonate and slowly wears the marble away", "The marble turns into metal", "The rain makes the marble whiter"]}
  answer={1}
>
Remember: **acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide**. Acid rain slowly dissolves marble and limestone, leaving the surface rough and pitted. This damage is sometimes called **"marble cancer"**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="How acid rain forms" />

<StepReveal>
<div>
Burning **coal and oil** in power stations, factories and refineries releases **sulfur dioxide** (from sulfur impurities in the fuel).
</div>
<div>
Hot vehicle engines and power stations also make **nitrogen oxides** (nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at high temperature).
</div>
<div>
These gases rise and dissolve in water droplets in clouds, forming **sulfuric acid** and **nitric acid**.
</div>
<div>
The acids fall as **acid rain** (pH below about 5.6), sometimes hundreds of kilometres from where the pollution was made!
</div>
</StepReveal>

<MythFact myth="Normal rain is perfectly neutral, pH 7.">
Even clean rain is **slightly acidic**, about **pH 5.6**, because carbon dioxide from the air dissolves in it to make weak carbonic acid. Rain is called **acid rain** only when pollution pushes it lower than this.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Protecting the Taj Mahal">
In 1996, India's Supreme Court created the **Taj Trapezium Zone**, an area of about 10,400 km² around the Taj Mahal. Coal-based industries there had to switch to cleaner fuels like natural gas or move away, and petrol and diesel vehicles are restricted near the monument. The marble is also cleaned with a special clay pack (*multani mitti*) to draw out dirt.
</RealLife>
