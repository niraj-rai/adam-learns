**About 100 times!** Each step on the pH scale is a **10× change**. From pH 4 to pH 2 is two steps: 10 × 10 = 100.

<Stage kind="explain" title="The pH scale" />

<ConceptCard title="pH from 0 to 14" emoji="📏">
The **pH scale** measures how acidic or basic a solution is:

- **pH below 7:** acidic. The lower the number, the **stronger** the acid.
- **pH 7:** neutral (pure water).
- **pH above 7:** basic. The higher the number, the **stronger** the base.

**Universal indicator** is a mixture of indicators that turns a different colour at each pH, from red (strong acid) through green (neutral) to purple (strong base). A **pH meter** measures it electronically.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="If you add enough water to an acid, it becomes basic.">
Adding water makes an acid **more dilute**, so its pH rises **towards 7**, but it never goes past 7. A dilute acid is still an acid. You need a **base** to push the pH above 7.
</MythFact>

<ConceptCard title="pH in everyday life" emoji="🌏">
- **Your body** works between pH 7.0 and 7.8. Blood is about 7.4.
- **Your stomach** makes hydrochloric acid (pH about 1–2) to digest food. Too much causes acidity, treated with an **antacid** (a mild base).
- **Teeth:** bacteria turn sugar into acids. Below pH 5.5, tooth enamel starts to dissolve, so we use **basic toothpaste**.
- **Soil:** most crops grow best around pH 6–7. Farmers add lime to acidic soil and organic matter to basic soil.
- **Bee and ant stings** inject acid (methanoic acid); baking soda eases the sting.
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
The "p" in pH stands for *potenz* (German for "power"). It was invented in **1909** by the Danish chemist **Søren Sørensen** while working at the Carlsberg laboratory, to check the quality of beer!
</DidYouKnow>
