<Hook emoji="🫙">
Your grandmother never stores achaar (pickle) or tamarind chutney in a metal tin. She always uses glass or ceramic jars. What could the sour pickle do to a metal container?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A piece of magnesium ribbon is dropped into dilute hydrochloric acid. What do you think happens?"
  options={["Nothing", "It fizzes and a gas is given off", "It turns into gold", "The acid freezes"]}
  answer={1}
>
It **fizzes**. The acid reacts with the metal, making a salt and **hydrogen gas**. That is why sour foods can slowly eat away at some metal containers and pick up a metallic taste.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Two important reactions" />

<Formula tex="\text{acid} + \text{metal} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{hydrogen}" />

<Formula tex="\text{acid} + \text{carbonate} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water} + \text{carbon dioxide}" />

<ConceptCard title="Naming the salt" emoji="🏷️">
The **first part** of the salt's name comes from the **metal**; the **second part** from the **acid**:

- hydro**chloric** acid → **chloride** salts (e.g. magnesium chloride)
- **sulfuric** acid → **sulfate** salts (e.g. zinc sulfate)
- **nitric** acid → **nitrate** salts (e.g. calcium nitrate)
- **acetic** acid (vinegar) → **acetate** salts (e.g. sodium acetate)
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="All metals react with acids.">
Some metals are too **unreactive**. **Copper**, **silver** and **gold** don't react with dilute acids. That's one reason gold jewellery stays shiny for centuries. You'll explore this "reactivity series" in Unit 5!
</MythFact>

<ConceptCard title="Useful salts at home" emoji="🏠">
- **Baking soda** (sodium hydrogen carbonate): makes dhokla and cakes fluffy. With an acid it releases CO₂ bubbles. It's also used in antacids.
- **Washing soda** (sodium carbonate): softens hard water and removes grease.
- **Plaster of Paris** (calcium sulfate): mixed with water it sets hard. Used for fracture casts, statues and decorations.
- **Bleaching powder** (calcium oxychloride): disinfects drinking water and bleaches cloth.
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
**Baking powder** is baking soda *plus* a dry acid (like tartaric acid). When water is added, the acid and carbonate react and release carbon dioxide, which makes idli, dhokla and cakes rise. Heat from cooking releases even more gas.
</DidYouKnow>
