<Stage kind="explain" title="What is an indicator?" />

<ConceptCard title="Indicators" emoji="🎨">
An **indicator** is a substance that shows a **different colour** in acids and bases. It doesn't change the substance being tested; it just "indicates" whether it is acidic, basic or neutral.

- **Natural indicators** come from plants: litmus (lichens), turmeric, china rose (hibiscus) petals, red cabbage.
- **Synthetic indicators** are made in labs: phenolphthalein, methyl orange, universal indicator.
</ConceptCard>

| Indicator | In acid | In base | Can it detect acids? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue litmus | turns **red** | stays blue | ✅ |
| Red litmus | stays red | turns **blue** | ❌ (detects bases) |
| Turmeric | stays yellow | turns **red-brown** | ❌ |
| China rose | turns **dark pink** | turns **green** | ✅ |
| Phenolphthalein | colourless | turns **pink** | ❌ |
| Methyl orange | turns **red** | turns **yellow** | ✅ |

<Lab id="indicator-lab" />

<MythFact myth="Turmeric turning red means there's an acid.">
Turmeric turns red in **bases**, not acids! In acids and in neutral solutions it simply **stays yellow**, so turmeric can't tell an acid from a neutral solution. That's why we often need more than one indicator.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
**Olfactory indicators** change **smell** instead of colour! Onion and vanilla extract lose their smell in a basic solution but keep it in an acid. These are useful for people who are visually impaired.
</DidYouKnow>
