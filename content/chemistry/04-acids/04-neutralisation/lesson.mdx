<Hook emoji="💊">
After a big plate of spicy chole bhature, your uncle complains of a burning feeling in his chest. He chews an antacid tablet and feels better in minutes. What did the tablet do to the acid in his stomach?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="When an acid and a base are mixed in the right amounts, you get…"
  options={["A stronger acid", "A salt and water", "Nothing, since they cancel out and disappear", "A poisonous gas"]}
  answer={1}
>
They react to form a **salt** and **water**, and the mixture becomes **neutral**. This is called **neutralisation**. The antacid (a mild base) neutralised the extra stomach acid.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Acid + base → salt + water" />

<Formula tex="\text{acid} + \text{base} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water} \quad (+\ \text{heat})" />

<ConceptCard title="Neutralisation" emoji="🤝">
In **neutralisation**, an acid and a base cancel out each other's effects. The products are a **salt** and **water**, and **heat is given out** (it's exothermic).

For example:

hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water

A **salt** in chemistry is any compound made when an acid reacts with a base. Table salt (sodium chloride) is just one of many salts!
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Titration: find the exact amount" />

<Lab id="neutralise-it" />

<ConceptCard title="What is a titration?" emoji="🧪">
A **titration** adds a solution drop by drop from a **burette** until the reaction is exactly complete: the **end point**, shown by an indicator changing colour.

Notice the graph: the pH changes slowly at first, then **jumps suddenly** near the end point. One single drop can change the pH from about 4 to 10! That's why chemists add the last few drops very carefully.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Once you add a base, the solution is automatically neutral.">
Only the **exact** amount makes it neutral. Add too little and it's still acidic; add too much and it becomes **basic**! Try overshooting in the titration lab. That's why doctors warn against taking too many antacids.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
**Wasp stings** are different: they are slightly **basic**, so people traditionally use a mild acid like vinegar on them. (If a sting causes swelling or breathing trouble, get medical help immediately.)
</DidYouKnow>
