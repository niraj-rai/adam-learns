<Hook emoji="🍋">
Why is nimbu pani sour, but soap feels slippery? Why does a spoon of antacid calm a burning stomach? It all comes down to two families of substances: **acids** and **bases**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Which of these is NOT an acid?"
  options={["Lemon juice", "Vinegar", "Soap solution", "Tamarind (imli) water"]}
  answer={2}
>
**Soap solution is a base.** Lemon juice (citric acid), vinegar (acetic acid) and tamarind (tartaric acid) are all acids. That's why they taste sour.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Test safely: never taste or touch!" emoji="⚠️">
Some acids and bases are **corrosive**: they can burn skin and eyes. Strong acids (like the sulfuric acid in car batteries) and strong bases (like drain cleaner) are dangerous.

Scientists identify them with **indicators**: substances that change colour in acids or bases. The most famous is **litmus**, made from lichens and sold as paper strips.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="kitchen-hunt" />

<MythFact myth="All acids are dangerous.">
You eat acids every day! Citric acid in lemons and oranges, lactic acid in curd, and vitamin C (ascorbic acid) in amla are all harmless and good for you. Only **strong, concentrated** acids are dangerous. Even your stomach makes hydrochloric acid to digest food!
</MythFact>

<MythFact myth="Bases are safe because they are the opposite of acids.">
Strong bases like **sodium hydroxide** (used in drain cleaners and soap-making) can burn skin just as badly as strong acids. "Opposite" does not mean "safe".
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
**Amla** (Indian gooseberry) is one of the richest natural sources of **vitamin C**: about 20 times more than an orange! Vitamin C is an acid: *ascorbic acid*.
</DidYouKnow>
