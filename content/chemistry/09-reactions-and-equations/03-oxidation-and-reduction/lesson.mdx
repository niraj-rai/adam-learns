<Hook emoji="🕵️">
A cut apple goes brown, iron gates rust, oily snacks go stale, and power stations burn coal. Behind all of them are **redox** reactions, where one substance gains oxygen as another loses it.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="In CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O, which substance loses oxygen?"
  options={["H₂", "CuO", "Cu", "H₂O"]}
  answer={1}
>
CuO loses oxygen: it is **reduced**. Hydrogen gains it: it is **oxidised**.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Everyday redox" emoji="🔩">
- <KeyTerm def="The slow eating away of a metal by reaction with air, water or acids, such as rusting of iron.">Corrosion</KeyTerm>: iron rusts (needs oxygen **and** water); prevented by painting, oiling, galvanising (zinc coating) or making alloys.
- <KeyTerm def="The oxidation of fats and oils in food, which makes it smell and taste bad.">Rancidity</KeyTerm>: fats and oils oxidise; slowed by antioxidants, airtight packing, refrigeration and nitrogen-filled packets.
</ConceptCard>
