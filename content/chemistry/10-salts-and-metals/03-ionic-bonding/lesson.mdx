<Hook emoji="⚡">
Sodium is a dangerous metal and chlorine a poisonous gas, but together they make the salt on your dinner table. The change comes from electrons moving from one atom to the other.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Why does molten salt conduct electricity, but solid salt doesn’t?"
  options={["Solid salt has no ions", "In the molten salt the ions can move", "Heat creates electrons", "It does conduct as a solid"]}
  answer={1}
>
Ions are fixed in a solid lattice but **free to move** when molten or dissolved.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Key ideas" />

<ConceptCard title="Ionic (electrovalent) bonds" emoji="⚡">
Metals lose their outer electrons to become positive ions; non-metals gain them to become negative ions. The strong attraction between opposite charges is an <KeyTerm def="The electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions formed by transfer of electrons.">ionic bond</KeyTerm>. The ions pack into a giant <KeyTerm def="A regular three-dimensional arrangement of particles (here, ions) repeated throughout a crystal.">lattice</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>
