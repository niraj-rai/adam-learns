<Hook emoji="🔦">
On a sunny morning, a beam of light streams through a window and you can see dust dancing in it. Shine a torch through a glass of salt water and you see nothing at all. Shine it through diluted milk and the beam glows. The difference is all about the **size of the particles**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Which of these will show a visible torch beam passing through it?"
  options={["Salt water", "Sugar solution", "Diluted milk", "Pure water"]}
  answer={2}
>
Milk contains tiny droplets of fat and protein, big enough to **scatter** light. Salt and sugar break up into particles far too small to do this.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="The Tyndall effect" emoji="✨">
The scattering of a beam of light by particles in a colloid or suspension is the <KeyTerm def="The scattering of a beam of light by the particles of a colloid (or suspension), which makes the path of the beam visible.">Tyndall effect</KeyTerm>. It is a quick test: solutions never show it.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Types of colloid" emoji="🌫️">
A colloid has a <KeyTerm def="The substance present as tiny particles spread through a colloid (e.g. the water droplets in fog).">dispersed phase</KeyTerm> spread through a <KeyTerm def="The substance in which the dispersed phase is spread (e.g. the air in fog).">dispersion medium</KeyTerm>:
- **Aerosol** (liquid or solid in gas): fog, clouds, smoke.
- **Emulsion** (liquid in liquid): milk, face cream.
- **Foam** (gas in liquid or solid): shaving cream, sponge.
- **Gel** (liquid in solid): jelly, cheese.
- **Sol** (solid in liquid): paint, ink, mud mixed very finely.
</ConceptCard>
