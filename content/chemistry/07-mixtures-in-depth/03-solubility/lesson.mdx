<Hook emoji="☕">
Keep adding sugar to cold water and eventually it just sits at the bottom. But hot chai can dissolve a lot more. There is a limit to how much of a solid can dissolve, and that limit changes with temperature. Chemists draw it as a **solubility curve**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="You keep stirring sugar into a glass of water until some stays at the bottom however long you stir. The solution is now…"
  options={["unsaturated", "a suspension", "a colloid", "saturated"]}
  answer={3}
>
It has dissolved as much sugar as it can at that temperature: it is **saturated**. Stirring only speeds up dissolving; it doesn't raise the limit.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Solubility" />

<ConceptCard title="Saturated solutions" emoji="🧂">
The <KeyTerm def="The maximum mass of a solute that dissolves in 100 g of solvent at a given temperature.">solubility</KeyTerm> of a substance is the maximum mass that dissolves in **100 g of water** at a given temperature. A solution holding that maximum is <KeyTerm def="A solution that cannot dissolve any more solute at that temperature.">saturated</KeyTerm>; one holding less is unsaturated.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Reading a solubility curve" emoji="📈">
A solubility curve plots solubility (g per 100 g water) against temperature.
- A point **on** the curve: saturated.
- A point **below**: unsaturated (more can dissolve).
- A point **above**: the extra solid won't dissolve (it stays at the bottom).
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife>
Salt pans along the Gujarat and Tamil Nadu coasts make salt by evaporating seawater in the sun. Pure crystals of medicines and sugar are made in factories by **crystallisation**: dissolving the impure solid in hot water, filtering, then cooling so pure crystals form.
</RealLife>
