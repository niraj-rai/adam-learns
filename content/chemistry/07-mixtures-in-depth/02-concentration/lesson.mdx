<Hook emoji="🧪">
A hospital drip bag says **0.9% sodium chloride**. A hand sanitiser says **70% alcohol**. A mouthwash, a fruit drink, a fertiliser: all tell you their **concentration**. Get it wrong and a medicine can be useless or even dangerous. Chemists have three simple ways to measure it.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="20 g of sugar is dissolved in 80 g of water. What is the concentration by mass?"
  options={["25%", "20%", "80%", "16%"]}
  answer={1}
>
Divide by the mass of the **solution** (20 + 80 = 100 g), not the water: 20 ÷ 100 × 100 = **20%**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Three ways to measure concentration" />

<Formula tex="\%\,(m/m) = \frac{\text{mass of solute}}{\text{mass of solution}} \times 100" caption="Used for solids dissolved in liquids, e.g. sugar syrup" />

<Formula tex="\%\,(m/v) = \frac{\text{mass of solute (g)}}{\text{volume of solution (mL)}} \times 100" caption="Used in medicine, e.g. 0.9% saline = 0.9 g salt per 100 mL" />

<Formula tex="\%\,(v/v) = \frac{\text{volume of solute}}{\text{volume of solution}} \times 100" caption="Used for liquids in liquids, e.g. 70% alcohol in sanitiser" />

<ConceptCard title="Remember" emoji="💡">
Mass of solution = mass of solute + mass of solvent. A **concentrated** solution has a lot of solute; a **dilute** one has a little. Adding more water (diluting) lowers the concentration but never changes the amount of solute.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife>
Oral rehydration solution (ORS) saves millions of lives from diarrhoea. It works only when the salt and sugar are at the right concentration: too strong and it can make dehydration worse. That's why ORS packets are made to be mixed with an exact volume of clean water.
</RealLife>
