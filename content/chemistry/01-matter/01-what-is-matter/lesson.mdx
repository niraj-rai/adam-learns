<Stage kind="explain" title="The scientist's definition" />

<ConceptCard title="Matter" emoji="🧱">
**Matter** is anything that **has mass** and **takes up space**.

- <KeyTerm def="The amount of 'stuff' in an object. Measured in grams (g) or kilograms (kg).">Mass</KeyTerm> is how much stuff something contains.
- <KeyTerm def="The amount of space something takes up. Measured in millilitres (mL), litres (L) or cubic centimetres (cm³).">Volume</KeyTerm> is how much space it takes up.

If something has **both**, it is matter. Rocks, water, milk, air, steam, your body: all matter.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="matter-or-not" />

<MythFact myth="Air weighs nothing.">
Air has mass! One cubic metre of air (a box about as big as a washing machine) has a mass of about **1.2 kg**.

The air in your classroom (7 m × 8 m × 3 m) has a mass of around **200 kg**. That's heavier than three of your classmates put together!
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Over 2,000 years ago, the Indian philosopher **Maharishi Kanad** suggested that all matter is made of tiny particles called **paramanu** that cannot be divided further. Ancient Indian thinkers also grouped all matter into **Panch Tatva**: earth, water, fire, air and space. Scientists today use a different model, but the idea of tiny particles was a brilliant guess!
</DidYouKnow>

<Stage kind="apply" title="Think like a scientist" />

<Inquiry criterion="B" title="Design an experiment: does air have mass?">
Imagine you have a metre ruler, some string, two identical balloons and a pin.

1. Blow up both balloons to the same size and tie them to opposite ends of the ruler.
2. Hang the ruler from its middle so that it balances.
3. **Pop one balloon** carefully with the pin (the pieces of rubber stay tied on).

**Predict:** which way will the ruler tip? Why?

**Your variables:** the *independent variable* is whether the balloon contains air. The *dependent variable* is which way the ruler tips. Keep the balloons, the string and the position the same: these are *controlled variables*.
</Inquiry>

<QuickCheck question="In the balloon experiment, the side with the full balloon goes DOWN. What does this show?" options={["Air has mass", "Balloons are heavy", "The ruler was broken", "Air takes up no space"]} answer={0}>
The side with the **air-filled** balloon is heavier, so the air inside must have mass. Air is matter!
</QuickCheck>

<TryAtHome
  title="The dry tissue trick"
  materials={["a glass", "a tissue or paper napkin", "a big bowl or bucket of water"]}
  safety="Wipe up any spilled water so nobody slips."
>
1. Scrunch the tissue and push it firmly into the bottom of the glass.
2. Turn the glass **upside down** and push it **straight down** into the water. Do not tilt it!
3. Lift it straight out and feel the tissue.

**What happens?** The tissue stays dry. The air trapped in the glass takes up space, so the water cannot get in.
</TryAtHome>
