<Stage kind="explain" title="What is diffusion?" />

<ConceptCard title="Diffusion" emoji="🌫️">
**Diffusion** is the spreading out and mixing of particles, from where there are **many of them** (high concentration) to where there are **fewer** (low concentration).

It happens **on its own**, with no stirring, because particles are **always moving randomly** and bumping into each other.

- Fastest in **gases** (lots of space and fast particles)
- Slower in **liquids**
- Extremely slow in **solids** (particles are fixed)
- Faster at **higher temperatures**
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Once the dye is evenly mixed, the particles stop moving.">
The particles **never stop moving**. After mixing, they keep moving randomly in all directions, but because they're spread evenly, the colour looks the same everywhere. Scientists call this a *dynamic* situation: lots of motion, no overall change.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
In 1827 the botanist **Robert Brown** saw pollen grains in water jiggling around under a microscope, even though nothing was pushing them. In 1905, **Albert Einstein** showed that the jiggling is caused by invisible water particles constantly hitting the pollen. This **Brownian motion** was some of the first strong evidence that particles are real!
</DidYouKnow>

<RealLife title="Diffusion keeps you alive">
Every breath you take, **oxygen** diffuses from your lungs into your blood (where there is less oxygen), and **carbon dioxide** diffuses the other way. You can also thank diffusion for spreading the smell of your neighbour's dosa, and the colour from a tea bag in hot water.
</RealLife>

<Stage kind="apply" title="Design your own investigation" />

<Inquiry criterion="B" title="Tea bag investigation">
**Research question:** *How does the temperature of water affect how fast the colour spreads from a tea bag?*

Plan your investigation:

- **Independent variable** (what you change): water temperature. Choose at least 3 values, e.g. 20 °C, 40 °C and 60 °C.
- **Dependent variable** (what you measure): the time taken for the water to become evenly coloured.
- **Controlled variables** (what you keep the same): volume of water, type of tea bag, size of cup, no stirring.
- **Hypothesis:** "If the water is hotter, then the colour will spread faster, because…" (finish it using the particle model!)
- **Safety:** use warm tap water, not boiling water.
</Inquiry>

<TryAtHome
  title="Hot vs cold colour race"
  materials={["2 identical clear glasses", "cold water", "warm tap water", "food colouring (or a drop of haldi water)"]}
  safety="Use warm tap water, not boiling water. Ask an adult to help."
>
1. Fill one glass with cold water and one with warm water, to the same level.
2. Let the water go completely still for a minute.
3. Gently add **one drop** of colouring to each at the same time. **Don't stir!**
4. Time how long each takes to look evenly coloured. Record your results in a table.
</TryAtHome>
