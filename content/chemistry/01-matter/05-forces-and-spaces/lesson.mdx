<Stage kind="explain" title="Forces and spaces" />

<ConceptCard title="Interparticle forces of attraction" emoji="🧲">
Particles **pull on each other**. These pulls are called <KeyTerm def="The forces that hold particles of a substance together. Strongest in solids, weakest in gases.">interparticle forces of attraction</KeyTerm>.

- **Solids:** strongest forces, so particles are held in place. Solids are **rigid** and hard to break.
- **Liquids:** weaker forces, so particles stay together but can slide.
- **Gases:** very weak forces, so particles fly apart freely.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Interparticle spaces" emoji="↔️">
The gaps between particles are called <KeyTerm def="The empty spaces between particles of matter. Largest in gases.">interparticle spaces</KeyTerm>.

- **Solids** and **liquids** have very small spaces, so they are **almost incompressible**.
- **Gases** have very large spaces, so they are **highly compressible**. Pushing moves the particles closer together.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="see" title="More evidence for spaces" />

<StepReveal>
<div>
Fill a glass with water **right to the brim** and mark the level.
</div>
<div>
Slowly stir in a spoon of sugar. Does the water overflow? **Hardly at all!**
</div>
<div>
The sugar breaks up into tiny particles that fit into the **spaces between the water particles**. It isn't destroyed: taste the water and it's sweet!
</div>
<div>
**Model it:** mix a cup of marbles with sand. The sand fills the gaps, and the total is less than the two volumes added together. (Real particles don't behave exactly like marbles, but the idea of spaces is the same.)
</div>
</StepReveal>

<MythFact myth="When you squash a gas, the particles get squashed smaller.">
The particles **stay the same size**. Only the **spaces between them** get smaller. Push hard enough and the particles end up touching, which is how LPG turns into a liquid inside the cylinder!
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Squashed gas all around you">
- **LPG** in kitchen cylinders and autos is stored under pressure as a liquid.
- **CNG** (compressed natural gas) powers many buses in India.
- **Oxygen cylinders** in hospitals hold thousands of litres of oxygen.
- Your **cycle tyre** stays firm because air is squeezed into it by the pump.
</RealLife>

<Inquiry criterion="C" title="Look for the pattern">
Use the Syringe Squeeze lab. Set the push force to 0, 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100 and record the **gas volume** each time.

1. Draw a table: *Push force* | *Gas volume (%)*.
2. Describe the pattern: does the volume go down by the **same amount** each time you add 20?
3. Suggest why it gets **harder and harder** to squeeze the gas as it gets smaller. (Hint: think about how close the particles are.)
