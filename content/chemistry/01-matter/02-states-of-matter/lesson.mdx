<Stage kind="explain" title="The particle model" />

<ConceptCard title="Three big ideas" emoji="⚛️">
Scientists explain the behaviour of matter with the **particle model**:

1. All matter is made of **tiny particles**.
2. The particles are **always moving**. The hotter they are, the faster they move.
3. There are **forces of attraction** between particles, and **spaces** between them.

The model is the same for every state. What changes is **how close** the particles are and **how much they move**.
</ConceptCard>

<QuickCheck
  question="Why can you pour water but not a brick?"
  options={["Water particles are smaller than brick particles", "Water particles can slide past each other", "Water has no particles", "Bricks are heavier"]}
  answer={1}
>
In a liquid, particles are close but **not fixed in place**, so they can slide past one another and the liquid flows. In a solid, the particles are held in fixed positions.
</QuickCheck>

<MythFact myth="The particles in a solid don't move at all.">
Solid particles are **always vibrating** on the spot, even in ice. Slide the simulator down to −40 °C and watch closely: the particles still jiggle. They can't move *past* each other, but they are never still.
</MythFact>

<MythFact myth="The spaces between gas particles are filled with air.">
There is **nothing at all** in the spaces between particles: no air, no water. Air *is* the particles (mostly nitrogen and oxygen). The gaps between them are empty space.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
There is a **fourth state of matter** called **plasma**, a super-hot gas where the particles are electrically charged. Lightning, the Sun and neon signs are all plasma. More than 99% of the visible matter in the Universe is plasma!
</DidYouKnow>

<RealLife title="Bengaluru mornings">
Coconut oil melts at about **24 °C**. On a cool Bengaluru morning (around 15 °C) it is below its melting point, so it is **solid**. By afternoon (about 28 °C) it is above its melting point, so it becomes **liquid**. Same particles, different amounts of movement!
</RealLife>
