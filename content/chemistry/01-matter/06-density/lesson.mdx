<PredictFirst
  question="Which is heavier: 1 kg of iron or 1 kg of cotton?"
  options={["1 kg of iron", "1 kg of cotton", "They weigh the same", "It depends on the weather"]}
  answer={2}
>
Trick question! They both have a mass of **1 kg**. But the cotton takes up **far more space**. The iron packs its mass into a small volume: it is **denser**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="What is density?" />

<ConceptCard title="Density" emoji="⚖️">
**Density** tells you how much mass is packed into a certain volume.

A substance whose particles are **heavy** and **tightly packed** has a **high density**.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="\text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}} \qquad \rho = \frac{m}{V}" caption="Units: g/cm³ (or kg/m³). Water has a density of 1 g/cm³." />

<StepReveal>
<div>
**Worked example.** A stone has a mass of **30 g** and a volume of **12 cm³**. What is its density?
</div>
<div>
Write the formula: density = mass ÷ volume
</div>
<div>
Substitute: density = 30 g ÷ 12 cm³
</div>
<div>
Answer: density = **2.5 g/cm³**. That is more than water (1 g/cm³), so the stone **sinks**.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<ConceptCard title="The float or sink rule" emoji="🛟">
- An object **floats** on a liquid if it is **less dense** than the liquid.
- An object **sinks** in a liquid if it is **more dense** than the liquid.

A steel ship floats because its shape holds a huge amount of **air**. The **average** density of steel + air is less than water. Squash the ship into a solid lump and it would sink like the nail!
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Heavy things sink and light things float.">
It's not about the **mass** alone, it's about **density**. A huge log (very heavy) floats on a river, while a tiny pebble (very light) sinks. The log is less dense than water; the pebble is more dense.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Ice is **less dense** than liquid water (0.92 g/cm³ compared with 1.00 g/cm³). That's unusual: most substances are densest as solids. Because ice floats, lakes freeze from the **top down**, and the ice layer protects the fish swimming in the liquid water below!
</DidYouKnow>

<RealLife title="Density in everyday life">
- **Egg test:** a fresh egg sinks in water; an old egg floats because air builds up inside it.
- **Oil spills** float on the sea because oil is less dense than water.
- Hot air **balloons** rise because hot air is less dense than the cooler air around it.
</RealLife>

<TryAtHome
  title="The floating egg"
  materials={["1 raw egg", "a tall glass of water", "salt", "a spoon"]}
>
1. Gently place the egg in plain water. It sinks.
2. Take it out, stir in 6–8 spoons of salt, and put the egg back.
3. Keep adding salt until the egg **floats**.

**Why?** Dissolving salt makes the water **denser**. Once the salt water is denser than the egg, the egg floats.
</TryAtHome>

<CbseCorner source="Class 9 Gravitation (floatation)">
- CBSE uses **relative density** = density of substance ÷ density of water. It has **no unit**.
- Unit conversion: **1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³**.
- Numericals like *"Find the density of a 50 g block with volume 20 cm³"* are standard 2-mark questions.
</CbseCorner>
