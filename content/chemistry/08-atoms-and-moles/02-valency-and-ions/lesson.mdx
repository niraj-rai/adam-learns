<Stage kind="explain" title="Shells and valency" />

<ConceptCard title="Electron arrangement" emoji="🔵">
For the first 20 elements, electrons fill shells in the order **2, 8, 8, 2**. Oxygen (8 electrons) is 2, 6; chlorine (17) is 2, 8, 7; calcium (20) is 2, 8, 8, 2. The electrons in the outermost shell are the <KeyTerm def="The electrons in the outermost shell of an atom. They take part in chemical reactions.">valence electrons</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Valency" emoji="🤝">
Atoms react to get a full outer shell (8 electrons, or 2 for the first shell) like the noble gases. <KeyTerm def="The combining capacity of an atom: the number of electrons it loses, gains or shares to get a full outer shell.">Valency</KeyTerm> is the number of electrons lost, gained or shared to do this.
- 1–3 outer electrons: **lose** them → valency = number of outer electrons.
- 5–7 outer electrons: **gain** (8 − outer) → valency = 8 − outer.
- 4 outer electrons: **share** (valency 4), e.g. carbon.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="When sodium becomes Na⁺, it gains a proton.">
The number of protons never changes in a chemical reaction; that would change the element! Na⁺ has 11 protons and only 10 electrons, so it has an overall charge of +1.
</MythFact>
