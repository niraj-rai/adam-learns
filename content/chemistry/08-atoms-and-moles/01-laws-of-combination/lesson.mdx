<Stage kind="wonder" title="Where does the wood go?" />

<Hook emoji="🔥">
A big log burns down to a small heap of ash. Has most of the matter vanished? In the 1770s Antoine Lavoisier weighed everything carefully, **including the gases**, and found that nothing is ever lost. A few years later Joseph Proust noticed that every pure compound always has the same recipe. These two laws led John Dalton to the idea of atoms.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="12 g of carbon burns completely in 32 g of oxygen. What mass of carbon dioxide forms?"
  options={["20 g", "12 g", "32 g", "44 g"]}
  answer={3}
>
Mass is conserved: 12 + 32 = **44 g**. Nothing is created or destroyed; the atoms are just rearranged.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Two laws" />

<ConceptCard title="Law of conservation of mass" emoji="⚖️">
In a chemical reaction, mass is neither created nor destroyed. **Total mass of reactants = total mass of products.** (If a gas escapes, it must be weighed too.)
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Law of constant proportions" emoji="💧">
A pure compound always contains the same elements combined in the **same proportion by mass**, wherever it comes from. Water is always 1 part hydrogen to 8 parts oxygen by mass; carbon dioxide is always 3 parts carbon to 8 parts oxygen.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Laws of Chemical Combination" />

<Lab id="constant-proportions" />

<ConceptCard title="Dalton's atomic theory (1808)" emoji="⚛️">
- All matter is made of tiny particles called **atoms**, which cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction.
- Atoms of one element are identical in mass and properties; atoms of different elements differ.
- Atoms combine in small **whole-number ratios** to form compounds.
- A chemical reaction **rearranges** atoms.

The first and last points explain conservation of mass; the third explains constant proportions.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Dalton got everything right.">
We now know atoms **can** be split (into protons, neutrons and electrons), and atoms of the same element can have different masses (isotopes). But Dalton's big idea, that matter is made of atoms that rearrange in reactions, is still at the heart of chemistry.
</MythFact>
