<Stage kind="explain" title="Key ideas" />

<ConceptCard title="Standard form" emoji="📐">
A quadratic equation is ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0. Its solutions are called **roots**.
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal
  steps={[
    "Hall: breadth x, length 2x + 1. So x(2x + 1) = 300, i.e. 2x² + x − 300 = 0.",
    "Split the middle term: find two numbers with product 2 × (−300) = −600 and sum 1: 25 and −24.",
    "2x² + 25x − 24x − 300 = x(2x + 25) − 12(2x + 25) = (x − 12)(2x + 25).",
    "x = 12 or x = −12.5. A breadth can’t be negative, so the hall is 12 m by 25 m."
  ]}
/>

<ConceptCard title="The quadratic formula" emoji="🧮">
When factorising is hard, use **x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a**. It comes from completing the square, a method known to Sridharacharya in the 11th century.
</ConceptCard>
