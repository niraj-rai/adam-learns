<Hook emoji="🎨">
You mix 2 cups of blue paint with 3 cups of yellow and get the perfect green for your room. Then you run out. To get *exactly* the same green, how much yellow do you mix with 6 cups of blue? Ratios tell you: the parts must grow together.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="2 blue : 3 yellow makes your green. What goes with 6 cups of blue?"
  options={["7 cups of yellow", "9 cups of yellow", "6 cups of yellow", "18 cups of yellow"]}
  answer={1}
>
Blue was multiplied by 3 (2 → 6), so yellow must be too: 3 × 3 = **9**. Adding 4 to both (6 : 7) would give a different shade.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Comparing parts" />

<ConceptCard title="What a ratio tells you">
A <KeyTerm def="A comparison of two or more quantities, written like 2 : 3">ratio</KeyTerm> compares quantities by how many **parts** of each. 2 : 3, 4 : 6 and 10 : 15 are <KeyTerm def="Ratios that simplify to the same simplest form, like 4 : 6 and 2 : 3">equivalent ratios</KeyTerm>. To simplify, divide every part by the HCF.
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal
  steps={[
    "Share ₹1200 between three friends in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5.",
    "Add the parts: 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 parts.",
    "Find one part: ₹1200 ÷ 10 = ₹120.",
    "Multiply: 2 × 120 = ₹240, 3 × 120 = ₹360, 5 × 120 = ₹600.",
    "Check: 240 + 360 + 600 = 1200 ✓."

<RealLife>
Every map has a **scale**, which is a ratio. On a 1 : 50,000 trekking map of the Western Ghats, 1 cm stands for 50,000 cm = 500 m in real life. Architects, model makers and Google Maps all rely on scale ratios.
</RealLife>
