<Hook emoji="🔍">
Type 1 ÷ 7 into a calculator: 0.142857142857… The same six digits come round again and again, forever. Type 1 ÷ 8 and you get 0.125, which simply stops. Why do some fractions stop and others repeat? And could a fraction ever go on forever **without** repeating?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Which fraction has a terminating (stopping) decimal?"
  options={["1/3", "1/6", "3/20", "2/7"]}
  answer={2}
>
20 = 2² × 5, so 3/20 = 15/100 = **0.15**. Denominators whose only prime factors are 2 and 5 give terminating decimals.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Stop or repeat" />

<ConceptCard title="Why decimals of fractions repeat" emoji="🔁">
When you divide p by q, each step leaves a remainder smaller than q. There are only q − 1 possible non-zero remainders, so either a remainder is 0 (the decimal **terminates**) or a remainder comes back, and from then on the digits repeat (the decimal <KeyTerm def="A decimal in which a block of digits repeats forever, e.g. 0.1666… or 0.142857142857…">recurs</KeyTerm>).
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="The rule" emoji="📏">
Write p/q in lowest terms. It terminates exactly when q = 2ᵐ × 5ⁿ. Otherwise it recurs.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="0.999… is a tiny bit less than 1.">
Let x = 0.999…; then 10x = 9.999…, so 9x = 9 and x = 1 **exactly**. Another way: 1/3 = 0.333…, so 3 × 1/3 = 0.999… = 1.
</MythFact>
