<Stage kind="explain" title="Beyond fractions" />

<ConceptCard title="Irrational numbers" emoji="♾️">
An <KeyTerm def="A number that cannot be written as p/q with p, q integers and q ≠ 0. Its decimal never ends and never repeats.">irrational number</KeyTerm> can't be written as p/q. Examples: √2, √3, √5, π. The rational and irrational numbers together make the <KeyTerm def="All the rational and irrational numbers: every point on the number line.">real numbers</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal
  steps={[
    "Suppose √2 = p/q, a fraction in lowest terms (p and q have no common factor).",
    "Square both sides: 2 = p²/q², so p² = 2q². So p² is even, which means p is even: p = 2k.",
    "Then (2k)² = 2q², so 4k² = 2q² and q² = 2k². So q is even too.",
    "But then p and q share the factor 2, contradicting 'lowest terms'. So √2 is irrational. ∎"
  ]}
/>

<Lab id="root-spiral" />

<MythFact myth="π = 22/7.">
22/7 = 3.142857…, while π = 3.14159265… They agree to only two decimal places. π is irrational: no fraction equals it exactly. Āryabhaṭa gave the better value 62832/20000 = 3.1416 in 499 CE.
</MythFact>
