<Hook emoji="📍">
How do you tell a friend exactly where to find something on a map, a chessboard, or a video game screen? You need **two numbers**: how far across and how far up. The mathematician and philosopher René Descartes made this idea famous, and every GPS, spreadsheet and computer game uses it today.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Are (2, 5) and (5, 2) the same point?"
  options={["Yes, they use the same numbers", "No, order matters: x first, then y", "Only in quadrant I", "It depends on the scale"]}
  answer={1}
>
(2, 5) is 2 across and 5 up; (5, 2) is 5 across and 2 up. Different points! That's why they're called **ordered pairs**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="The Cartesian plane" />

<ConceptCard title="Axes, origin and quadrants">
The horizontal <KeyTerm def="The horizontal number line on a graph">x-axis</KeyTerm> and vertical y-axis cross at the <KeyTerm def="The point (0, 0) where the axes cross">origin</KeyTerm>. A point (x, y) is found by going x across, then y up or down. The axes split the plane into four **quadrants**, numbered I to IV anticlockwise from the top right.
</ConceptCard>
