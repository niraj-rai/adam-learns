<Stage kind="explain" title="Two powerful theorems" />

<ConceptCard title="Remainder theorem" emoji="🧩">
When p(x) is divided by (x − a), the remainder is **p(a)**. Why? p(x) = (x − a)·q(x) + r. Put x = a: the first part becomes 0, leaving p(a) = r.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Factor theorem" emoji="✅">
(x − a) is a factor of p(x) **exactly when p(a) = 0**. To find factors, try values of a that divide the constant term.
</ConceptCard>
