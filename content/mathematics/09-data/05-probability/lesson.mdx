<Hook emoji="🔢">
Adam and his sister roll two dice. Adam wins if the total is 7; his sister wins if it's 6 or 8. Two numbers beat one, so she's obviously more likely to win… right? To be sure, you need to list the **sample space**, every possible outcome, and count.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="With two dice, which total is most likely?"
  options={["2", "7", "12", "They're all equally likely"]}
  answer={1}
>
**7** can be made 6 ways (1+6, 2+5, 3+4, 4+3, 5+2, 6+1), more than any other total. Only one way makes 2 or 12.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Counting outcomes" />

<Formula tex="P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of equally likely outcomes}}" caption="Theoretical probability" />

<StepReveal
  steps={[
    "List the sample space: two dice give 6 × 6 = 36 equally likely outcomes.",
    "Mark the outcomes in the event: a total of 7 happens in 6 of them.",
    "P(total is 7) = 6/36 = 1/6.",
    "Complement: P(not 7) = 1 − 1/6 = 5/6.",
    "Fair game? The sister's totals 6 and 8 happen 5 + 5 = 10 ways, so she has 10/36 vs Adam's 6/36. It's not fair."
