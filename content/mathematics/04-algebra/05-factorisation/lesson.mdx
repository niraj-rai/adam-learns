You know that (x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 5x + 6. What if you're given x² + 5x + 6 and asked which brackets made it? That's **factorisation**: finding the length and width of a rectangle when you only know its area.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst

3 × 4 = 12 and 3 + 4 = 7. So x² + 7x + 12 = **(x + 3)(x + 4)**.
</PredictFirst>

<StepReveal
  steps={[
    "Common factor: find the HCF of all the terms. 6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3); 4x²y + 6xy² = 2xy(2x + 3y).",
    "Difference of squares: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b). So x² − 16 = (x + 4)(x − 4).",
    "Quadratics x² + bx + c: find m and n with m × n = c and m + n = b.",
    "Example: x² − x − 6. Pairs multiplying to −6: (−6, 1), (−3, 2), (−2, 3), (−1, 6). Which adds to −1? −3 + 2.",
    "So x² − x − 6 = (x − 3)(x + 2). Always check by expanding!"
  ]}

Expand it: (x + 5)(x + 6) = x² + 11x + 30. The numbers in the brackets must **multiply** to 6 and **add** to 5: they are 2 and 3.
