<Hook emoji="🌳">
Chemists know that every substance is built from atoms. Mathematicians have their own atoms: the **prime numbers**. Every whole number bigger than 1 is built by multiplying primes together, in exactly one way. Your phone even relies on this: online security uses numbers made from two giant primes that are almost impossible to split apart.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst

A **prime** has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. The number 1 has only one factor, so it is **not** prime. (If 1 were prime, prime factorisations wouldn't be unique: 6 = 2 × 3 = 1 × 2 × 3 = 1 × 1 × 2 × 3…)
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="HCF and LCM from primes">
The <KeyTerm def="Highest Common Factor: the biggest number that divides both">HCF</KeyTerm> multiplies the primes the two numbers **share**. The <KeyTerm def="Lowest Common Multiple: the smallest number both divide into">LCM</KeyTerm> multiplies **all** the primes, counting shared ones once. For 12 = 2 × 2 × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3 × 3: HCF = 2 × 3 = 6, LCM = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 36.
</ConceptCard>
