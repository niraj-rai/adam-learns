<Hook emoji="✂️">
Draw any four-sided shape, as wonky as you like. Mark the midpoint of each side and join them up. You'll always get a **parallelogram**. The reason is a simple but powerful result about triangles: the midpoint theorem.
</Hook>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Parallelograms and midpoints" />

<ConceptCard title="Parallelogram properties" emoji="▱">
In a <KeyTerm def="A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.">parallelogram</KeyTerm>: opposite sides are equal, opposite angles are equal, adjacent angles add to 180°, and the diagonals **bisect each other**. Rectangles, rhombuses and squares are special parallelograms. Each property can be proved by drawing a diagonal and using congruent triangles.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="The midpoint theorem" emoji="✂️">
The line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is **parallel to the third side** and **half its length**. Converse: a line through the midpoint of one side, parallel to a second side, bisects the third side.
</ConceptCard>
