<Stage kind="wonder" title="Can dividing make things bigger?" />

<Hook emoji="🎀">
You have 3 m of ribbon and cut it into pieces 1/2 m long. You get **6** pieces. You divided 3, and got 6: a bigger number! Multiplying and dividing with fractions can surprise you. Let's find out why.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="What is 1/2 × 1/3?"
  options={["1/6", "2/5", "1/5", "5/6"]}
  answer={0}
>
“1/2 × 1/3” means **half of a third**. Cut a third in half and you get a sixth. Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes things **smaller**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Multiply and divide" />

<ConceptCard title="Multiplying: a fraction of a fraction">
Multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators: 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2. In an area model, shade 2/3 of the rows and 3/4 of the columns; the overlap is the answer.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Dividing: how many fit?">
3/4 ÷ 1/8 asks “how many eighths fit into three quarters?” The answer is 6. To divide, multiply by the <KeyTerm def="The fraction flipped upside down; a number times its reciprocal is 1">reciprocal</KeyTerm>: **keep, change, flip**: 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 6.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}" caption="Dividing is multiplying by the reciprocal" />

<MythFact myth="Multiplying always makes a number bigger, and dividing always makes it smaller.">
Only when you multiply or divide by numbers **greater than 1**. Multiply by 1/2 and you halve; divide by 1/2 and you double.
</MythFact>
