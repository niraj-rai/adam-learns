1/20 = 0.05. The denominator 20 = 2 × 2 × 5 uses only 2s and 5s, the prime factors of 10. The others have a factor of 3 or 7, so their decimals repeat.

<Stage kind="explain" title="Rational numbers" />

<ConceptCard title="What is a rational number?">
A <KeyTerm def="A number that can be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q is not 0">rational number</KeyTerm> can be written as a fraction p/q of two integers (q ≠ 0). That includes integers (5 = 5/1), negative fractions (−3/4), and decimals that stop or repeat (0.25 = 1/4, 0.333… = 1/3).
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Always another one in between">
Between any two rational numbers there is always another: their **average**. Between 1/3 and 1/2 is 5/12; between 1/3 and 5/12 is 3/8; and so on forever. We say the rational numbers are <KeyTerm def="Between any two, you can always find another">dense</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
0.999… (nines forever) is **exactly** equal to 1. One proof: 1/3 = 0.333…, so 3 × 1/3 = 0.999…, and 3 × 1/3 = 1.
</DidYouKnow>
