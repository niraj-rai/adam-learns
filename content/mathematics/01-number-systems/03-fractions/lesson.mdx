<Hook emoji="🍕">
Adam cuts his pizza into 4 slices and eats 2. His cousin cuts an identical pizza into 8 slices and eats 4. Who ate more? Neither! 2/4 and 4/8 look different, but they are the **same amount**, and both equal 1/2.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Which is bigger: 1/6 or 1/8?"
  options={["1/8, because 8 is bigger", "1/6, because the pieces are bigger", "They are equal", "It depends on the pizza"]}
  answer={1}
>
Cutting a whole into **more** pieces makes each piece **smaller**. So 1/6 is bigger than 1/8.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Equivalent fractions and adding" />

<ConceptCard title="Equivalent fractions">
Multiply (or divide) the <KeyTerm def="The top number: how many pieces you have">numerator</KeyTerm> and the <KeyTerm def="The bottom number: how many equal pieces make one whole">denominator</KeyTerm> by the same number, and the fraction's value doesn't change: 2/3 = 4/6 = 6/9 = 8/12.
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal
  steps={[

    "Now add the pieces: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.",
    "Never add the bottoms! 1/3 + 1/4 is NOT 2/7 (that's smaller than 1/3)."

<DidYouKnow>
Ancient Egyptians wrote almost every fraction as a sum of **unit fractions** (fractions with 1 on top): for example, 3/4 = 1/2 + 1/4. The Rhind papyrus (about 1550 BCE) has a whole table of them!
</DidYouKnow>
