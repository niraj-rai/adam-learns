<Hook emoji="📌">
Stretch a rubber band around pegs on a board to make any polygon: a star, an arrow, a strange zig-zag. Can you find its area just by **counting the pegs**? In 1899, the Austrian mathematician Georg Pick showed that you can, with an astonishingly simple formula.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="How would you find the area of an L-shaped room?"
  options={["It can't be done without a special formula", "Split it into rectangles and add them", "Multiply the two longest sides", "Measure the perimeter"]}
  answer={1}
>
Split it into two rectangles and **add** their areas, or take a big rectangle and **subtract** the missing corner. Both give the same answer.
</PredictFirst>

<DidYouKnow>
Pick's theorem only works on a grid of **squares** (or parallelograms). On a grid of triangles or in 3D, mathematicians had to find other formulas, and in 3D a simple version doesn't exist at all.
</DidYouKnow>
