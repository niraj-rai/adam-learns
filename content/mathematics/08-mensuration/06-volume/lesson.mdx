<Hook emoji="💧">
The water tank on the roof, a lunch box, a pressure cooker: they all hold something. **Volume** measures how much space a solid takes up, and **capacity** tells you how much it can hold. Engineers who design dams, water tanks and even space rockets are masters of volume.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="How many 1 cm cubes fit in a box 4 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm?"
  options={["9", "24", "12", "52"]}
  answer={1}
>
One layer holds 4 × 3 = 12 cubes, and there are 2 layers: **24 cubes**. Volume = l × b × h = 24 cm³.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Layers of base area" />

<Formula tex="V_{\text{cuboid}} = l \times b \times h \qquad V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi r^2 h \qquad 1\,\text{L} = 1000\,\text{cm}^3" caption="Volume = base area × height" />

<StepReveal
  steps={[
    "Count the cubes in one layer: that's the base area (l × b).",
    "Count the layers: that's the height (h).",
    "Volume = base area × height. This works for ANY prism, including cylinders (base πr²).",
    "Units: cm × cm × cm = cm³. 1000 cm³ = 1 litre; 1 m³ = 1000 litres.",
