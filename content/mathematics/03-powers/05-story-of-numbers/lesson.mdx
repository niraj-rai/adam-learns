<Hook emoji="🏛️">
Try multiplying MCMXLVII by XLII in Roman numerals. It's a nightmare! Now try 1947 × 42. Easy, with the method you learned in primary school. The difference is **place value with zero**, an idea perfected in India about 1,500 years ago that changed the world.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="What does the digit 3 stand for in 2,350?"
  options={["3", "30", "300", "3,000"]}
  answer={2}
>
In place value, a digit's value depends on its **position**: the 3 is in the hundreds place, so it means 300. In Roman numerals, C always means 100 wherever it is. There is no place value.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Every column is a power">
In base 10, the columns are 10⁰, 10¹, 10², 10³, …: 2026 = 2 × 10³ + 0 × 10² + 2 × 10¹ + 6 × 10⁰. In <KeyTerm def="Base 2: the only digits are 0 and 1, and each column is a power of 2">binary</KeyTerm>, the columns are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, …, and computers store everything this way.
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
The Indian place-value numerals spread to the Arab world and then to Europe. In 1202 the Italian mathematician Fibonacci's book *Liber Abaci* taught Europeans to use them, and slowly Roman numerals were replaced.
</DidYouKnow>
