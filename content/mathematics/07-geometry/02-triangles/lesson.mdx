<Hook emoji="🔺">
Draw any triangle: tall and thin, short and wide, lopsided. Tear off its three corners and line them up. They always make a perfectly straight line: **180°**. Why does every triangle, of every shape and size, obey this rule?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A huge triangle is drawn across a football field. What do its angles add up to?"
  options={["More than 180°", "Exactly 180°", "Less than 180°", "It depends on the lengths"]}
  answer={1}
>
Size doesn't matter: angles measure **turn**, not length. Every flat triangle's angles add to exactly 180°.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Why 180°?" />

<StepReveal
  steps={[
    "Take triangle ABC. Through C, draw a line parallel to AB.",

    "The angle between this line and CB equals ∠B (alternate angles).",
    "These two angles and ∠C sit together on a straight line, so ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°.",

<DidYouKnow>
On a globe, a triangle made of curved lines can have angles adding to **more** than 180°. Start at the North Pole, go down to the equator, a quarter of the way round, and back up: that triangle has three right angles! Flat (plane) geometry is special.
</DidYouKnow>
