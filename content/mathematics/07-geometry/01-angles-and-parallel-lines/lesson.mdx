<Hook emoji="🛤️">
Railway tracks must stay exactly parallel for hundreds of kilometres. Where a road crosses them, it makes eight angles, but look closely and you'll see only **two different sizes**. Engineers use these angle patterns to check that lines really are parallel.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Two lines cross, and one angle is 40°. What is the angle directly opposite it?"
  options={["140°", "40°", "50°", "320°"]}
  answer={1}
>
**Vertically opposite** angles are equal: 40°. The angles next to it form straight lines with it, so they are 180° − 40° = 140°.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Only when parallel">
These three rules work **only** when the lines are parallel. Turn it around: if a pair of corresponding (or alternate) angles is equal, the lines **must** be parallel. That's how carpenters and surveyors test for parallel lines.
</ConceptCard>
