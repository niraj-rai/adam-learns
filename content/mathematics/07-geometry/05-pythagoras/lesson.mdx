<Hook emoji="📏">
More than 2,500 years ago, Indian priests building fire altars needed perfect right angles. The **Baudhāyana Śulba Sūtra** describes how: a rope stretched along the diagonal of a rectangle makes an area equal to the areas made by the two sides together. Centuries later, the Greeks credited Pythagoras. Today we write it simply as **a² + b² = c²**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 3 and 4. How long is the longest side?"
  options={["7", "5", "6", "12"]}
  answer={1}
>
3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, and √25 = **5**. The square on the longest side has the same area as the other two squares together.
</PredictFirst>

<RealLife>
Builders still use the **3-4-5 rule**: mark 3 m along one wall and 4 m along the other; if the diagonal is exactly 5 m, the corner is a perfect right angle. GPS, screen sizes and ramp designs all use the same theorem.
</RealLife>
