<Hook emoji="🧫">
Under the microscope, onion cells look like neat bricks in a wall, while your cheek cells look like fried eggs scattered on a plate. Both are cells, both are alive, and both have a nucleus. So what makes a plant cell different from an animal cell?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Which of these does a plant cell have but an animal cell doesn't?"
  options={["A nucleus", "A cell membrane", "A cell wall", "Cytoplasm"]}
  answer={2}
>
The **cell wall**, a tough outer layer made of cellulose. Plant cells also have **chloroplasts** and a **large central vacuole**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Parts of a cell" />

<ConceptCard title="In every cell (plant and animal)" emoji="🔵">
- **Cell membrane**: a thin, flexible boundary that controls what goes in and out.
- **Cytoplasm**: jelly-like fluid where most chemical reactions happen.
- **Nucleus**: the control centre, containing DNA, the instructions for the cell.
- **Mitochondria**: release energy from food by respiration.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Extra parts in plant cells" emoji="🌿">
- **Cell wall**: made of cellulose; supports the cell and keeps its shape.
- **Chloroplasts**: contain green chlorophyll; make food by photosynthesis.
- **Large central vacuole**: filled with cell sap; keeps the cell firm.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="All plant cells have chloroplasts.">
Only plant cells that get light, mainly in leaves and green stems, have many chloroplasts. Root cells and onion bulb cells grow in the dark and have none.
</MythFact>
