<Stage kind="wonder" title="How does a plant grow taller?" />

<Hook emoji="🌱">
If you paint a mark on a young tree trunk 1 m above the ground, where will it be in ten years? Still at 1 m! Plants don't grow all over like we do. They grow at special places made of dividing cells, and the rest of the plant is made of cells that have become specialists.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A plant grows taller. Where are new cells being added?"
  options={["Evenly all along the stem", "Only at the base", "At the tips of shoots and roots", "In the leaves only"]}
  answer={2}
>
Growth in length happens at the **apical meristems** at the tips. That's why the mark on the trunk stays at the same height.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Two kinds of plant tissue" />

<ConceptCard title="Meristematic tissue" emoji="🔁">
<KeyTerm def="Plant tissue made of cells that keep dividing to produce new cells. Found at tips (apical), in the cambium (lateral) and at the bases of leaves (intercalary).">Meristematic tissue</KeyTerm> has small, actively dividing cells with thin walls, dense cytoplasm and big nuclei. **Apical** meristem at tips makes plants longer; **lateral** meristem (cambium) makes them thicker; **intercalary** meristem at nodes helps grasses regrow after grazing.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife>
Jute (grown mainly in West Bengal and Assam) and coconut coir are sclerenchyma fibres. Sugarcane's sweetness is sugar carried by phloem and stored in parenchyma. Bamboo and grass grow back quickly after cutting thanks to their intercalary meristems.
</RealLife>
