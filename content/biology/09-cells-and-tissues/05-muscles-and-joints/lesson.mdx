<Stage kind="wonder" title="Muscles can only pull" />

<Hook emoji="💪">
Hold a book and bend your arm: feel the front of your upper arm bulge. Straighten it: now the back tightens. A muscle can only **pull** (contract); it can never push. So how do we straighten a bent arm? With a second muscle pulling the other way.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="When you straighten your arm, which muscle contracts?"
  options={["The biceps", "The triceps", "Both at once", "Neither: the arm falls straight"]}
  answer={1}
>
The **triceps** at the back of the upper arm contracts and pulls the forearm straight, while the biceps relaxes. They are an **antagonistic pair**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="The musculoskeletal system" />

<ConceptCard title="Antagonistic pairs" emoji="↔️">
Muscles work in <KeyTerm def="Two muscles that work against each other at a joint: when one contracts, the other relaxes.">antagonistic pairs</KeyTerm>: biceps and triceps at the elbow, hamstrings and quadriceps at the knee. <KeyTerm def="Tough, non-stretchy cords of connective tissue that attach muscles to bones.">Tendons</KeyTerm> join muscles to bones; <KeyTerm def="Strong, slightly elastic bands of connective tissue that hold bones together at joints.">ligaments</KeyTerm> join bones to bones.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="muscle-pairs" />

<ConceptCard title="Healthy bones and muscles" emoji="🥛">
- **Calcium** (milk, curd, ragi, sesame, green leafy vegetables) and **vitamin D** (made in the skin in sunlight) build strong bones.
- **Protein** builds muscle.
- **Weight-bearing exercise** (running, skipping, sports) makes bones denser. Most bone mass is built by your early twenties, so your teenage habits matter for life.
- Good posture and warming up protect joints.
</ConceptCard>
