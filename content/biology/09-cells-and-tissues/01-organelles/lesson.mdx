<Hook emoji="🏭">
Each of your cells is only about a hundredth of a millimetre across, yet inside it thousands of jobs happen every second: instructions are read, proteins are built, energy is released, waste is recycled. Like a factory, it has departments. We call them **organelles**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Plant cells make their own food by photosynthesis. Do they also need mitochondria?"
  options={["No, chloroplasts do everything", "Yes, to release energy from glucose by respiration", "Only at night", "Only in the roots"]}
  answer={1}
>
Chloroplasts **make** glucose; mitochondria **release energy** from it. Plant cells respire all the time, day and night, so they need both.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Lysosomes: the clean-up crew" emoji="🧹">
<KeyTerm def="Small membrane sacs of digestive enzymes that break down worn-out organelles, food particles and germs.">Lysosomes</KeyTerm> digest old organelles and invading bacteria. If the cell is badly damaged they can burst and digest the cell itself, which is why they are nicknamed "suicide bags".
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife>
Cells that do a lot of one job have lots of the matching organelle: muscle and sperm cells are packed with mitochondria; pancreas cells that make enzymes are full of rough ER and Golgi; white blood cells that destroy germs contain many lysosomes.
</RealLife>

<DidYouKnow>
Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own small loops of DNA, a clue that they may once have been free-living bacteria that moved into bigger cells over a billion years ago.
</DidYouKnow>
