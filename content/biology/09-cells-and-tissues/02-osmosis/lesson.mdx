<Stage kind="wonder" title="Why do salted cucumbers go limp?" />

<Hook emoji="🥒">
Sprinkle salt on cucumber slices for a salad and after a few minutes they sit in a puddle of water and go floppy. Soak raisins in water overnight and they swell up plump. Water is moving in and out of the cells, all by itself.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Raisins are soaked in plain water overnight. What happens?"
  options={["They shrink", "They swell as water moves in", "They dissolve", "Nothing"]}
  answer={1}
>
The inside of a raisin is full of concentrated sugar. Water moves **into** its cells by osmosis, so it swells.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Moving across membranes" />

<ConceptCard title="Diffusion" emoji="🌬️">
<KeyTerm def="The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, until they are evenly spread.">Diffusion</KeyTerm> is how oxygen enters cells and carbon dioxide leaves. It needs no energy from the cell.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Osmosis" emoji="💧">
<KeyTerm def="The net movement of water molecules from a dilute solution (more water) to a concentrated solution (less water) through a partially permeable membrane.">Osmosis</KeyTerm> is a special case of diffusion: the movement of **water** through a <KeyTerm def="A membrane that lets some molecules (like water) through but not others (like sugar).">partially permeable membrane</KeyTerm>, such as the cell membrane.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Osmosis Lab" />

<Lab id="osmosis-lab" />

<RealLife>
Pickles and jams stay fresh because the high concentration of salt or sugar pulls water out of any bacteria by osmosis, so they can't grow. Drips in hospitals use 0.9% saline, isotonic with blood, so red blood cells neither swell nor shrink.
</RealLife>
