<Stage kind="explain" title="Five groups" />

<ConceptCard title="The main groups of microorganisms" emoji="🔬">
- **Bacteria**: tiny single cells with no nucleus, e.g. *Lactobacillus* (curd), *Rhizobium* (soil).
- **Fungi**: yeast (single-celled) and moulds (threads), e.g. bread mould.
- **Protozoa**: single cells with a nucleus that often move about, e.g. *Amoeba*, *Paramecium*, *Plasmodium*.
- **Algae**: plant-like, make food by photosynthesis, e.g. *Chlamydomonas*, *Spirogyra*.
- **Viruses**: not cells at all; they can only reproduce **inside** living cells, e.g. flu and polio.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="All microbes are germs that make us sick.">
Only a small fraction are <KeyTerm def="A microorganism that causes disease.">pathogens</KeyTerm>. Most microbes are harmless or helpful: they make oxygen (algae), recycle waste, fix nitrogen, make curd and idli, and even protect us from harmful microbes in our gut.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Tiny algae in the oceans make a large share of the oxygen we breathe, perhaps around half!
</DidYouKnow>

<CbseCorner source="Class 8 Curiosity Ch 2 and earlier NCERT Class 8 Ch 2">
- Microorganisms: **bacteria, fungi, protozoa, algae** (and viruses, which reproduce only inside host cells).
- They live everywhere: soil, water, air, and inside other organisms.
- Unicellular (bacteria, some algae, protozoa) and multicellular (moulds, some algae).
</CbseCorner>
