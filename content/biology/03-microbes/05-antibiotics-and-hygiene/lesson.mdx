<Hook emoji="💊">
In 1928, Alexander Fleming came back from holiday and found that mould had grown on one of his plates of bacteria. Around the mould, the bacteria had died. That lucky accident led to **penicillin**, the first antibiotic, which has since saved many millions of lives. But today, some bacteria are fighting back...
</Hook>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Fighting harmful microbes" />

<ConceptCard title="Antibiotics and resistance" emoji="💊">
**Antibiotics** kill bacteria or stop them multiplying. But when antibiotics are overused, or a course isn't finished, the toughest bacteria survive and multiply. Over time, **antibiotic-resistant** bacteria spread, and infections become harder to treat. That's why antibiotics should only be taken when a doctor prescribes them, and the full course should be finished.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Vaccines" emoji="💉">
A **vaccine** contains a weakened, killed or harmless part of a pathogen. It trains your immune system to recognise it, so your body can fight off the real infection quickly. Vaccination made India **polio-free** in 2014 and wiped out smallpox worldwide.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Hygiene: the first line of defence" emoji="🧼">
Many diseases spread through dirty hands, water and food: diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid and hepatitis A. Simple habits prevent them:
- Wash hands with **soap for 20 seconds** before eating and after using the toilet.
- Drink safe, boiled or filtered water.
- Cover coughs and sneezes.
- Keep water from collecting, to stop mosquitoes breeding (malaria, dengue).
</ConceptCard>
