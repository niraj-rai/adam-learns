<Stage kind="explain" title="Classifying life" />

<ConceptCard title="Five kingdoms (Whittaker, 1969)" emoji="👑">
- **Monera**: single cells with no nucleus (<KeyTerm def="A cell with no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles, like a bacterium.">prokaryotes</KeyTerm>), e.g. bacteria.
- **Protista**: single cells with a nucleus (<KeyTerm def="A cell with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.">eukaryotes</KeyTerm>), e.g. Amoeba, Paramecium, diatoms.
- **Fungi**: absorb food from dead matter; cell walls of chitin, e.g. mushrooms, yeast, moulds.
- **Plantae**: many-celled, make food by photosynthesis, cellulose walls.
- **Animalia**: many-celled, eat food, no cell walls, most can move.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="The ladder of ranks" emoji="🪜">
Kingdom → Phylum (Division for plants) → Class → Order → Family → Genus → **Species**. A <KeyTerm def="A group of organisms that are similar and can breed together to produce fertile offspring.">species</KeyTerm> is the basic unit: members can breed together and have fertile young.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Binomial nomenclature" emoji="📝">
Carl Linnaeus (1700s) gave each species a two-part Latin name: ***Genus species***.
1. The genus starts with a **capital** letter; the species is all **lower case**.
2. It is printed in *italics*, or underlined when handwritten.
3. e.g. *Homo sapiens* (humans), *Mangifera indica* (mango), *Panthera tigris* (tiger).
</ConceptCard>
