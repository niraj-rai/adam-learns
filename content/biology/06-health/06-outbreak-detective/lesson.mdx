<Hook emoji="🗺️">
In 1854, a terrible cholera outbreak struck London. Most people believed it was caused by "bad air". A doctor named **John Snow** marked every death on a map and noticed they clustered around one water pump on Broad Street. He persuaded officials to remove the pump's handle, and the outbreak faded. He had invented **epidemiology**. Now it's your turn.
</Hook>

<ConceptCard title="Your toolkit" emoji="🧰">
- **Identify** the disease from symptoms and tests.
- Work out **how it spreads**: air, water, contact or vectors.
- **Map** the cases: clusters point to a source.
- **Remove the source** and **treat** patients (ORS for diarrhoea).
- **Educate** the community to prevent new cases.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="Disease surveillance in India">
India's Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme collects reports of diseases like cholera, dengue and malaria from hospitals and health centres, so rising cases can be spotted early and teams sent to investigate, just like you did.
</RealLife>

<CbseCorner source="Class 8 Curiosity Ch 3">
Exam questions often ask how to prevent diseases spread by water, food or mosquitoes. Name the **route** first, then give a matching **prevention** (boiled water and toilets for cholera; removing standing water for dengue).
</CbseCorner>
