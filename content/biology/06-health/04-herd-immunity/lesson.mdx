<Hook emoji="👶">
A newborn baby is too young for the measles vaccine. A child being treated for cancer has a weakened immune system. How can we keep them safe from diseases that spread so easily? The answer is everyone else.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="If 50% of a town is vaccinated against measles, what happens when a case arrives?"
  options={["Nobody gets ill", "A large outbreak can still spread", "Only vaccinated people get ill", "The virus dies immediately"]}
  answer={1}
>
Measles is so contagious (each case can infect about 15 others) that around **95%** of people must be immune to stop it spreading. At 50%, a big outbreak can still happen.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="R₀ and herd immunity" />

<ConceptCard title="R₀" emoji="📈">
<KeyTerm def="The average number of people one infected person infects, in a population where no one is immune.">R₀</KeyTerm> (say "R-nought") tells us how contagious a disease is. If R₀ is 3, one case leads to 3, then 9, then 27... The outbreak grows **exponentially**, until people become immune.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="\text{herd-immunity threshold} = 1 - \frac{1}{R_0}" caption="e.g. measles: 1 − 1/15 ≈ 93%" />

<Stage kind="try" title="Outbreak Simulator" />

<Lab id="outbreak-sim" />

<ConceptCard title="Herd immunity" emoji="🐄">
When enough people are immune, each case infects **fewer than one** new person on average, and the outbreak dies out. The germ can't reach those who aren't immune, like babies and people with weak immune systems. That's <KeyTerm def="Protection of a whole community when a large enough share of people are immune, so a disease can't spread easily.">herd immunity</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>
