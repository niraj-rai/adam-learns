<Hook emoji="🌳">
A tiny mango seed grows into a tree weighing tonnes. Where does all that mass come from? The soil? Around 1640, Jan van Helmont grew a willow tree in a pot for five years. The tree gained about 74 kg, but the soil lost only about 60 grams! So what is a tree actually made of?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Most of a tree's mass comes from..."
  options={["The soil", "Carbon dioxide from the air (and water)", "Sunlight", "Fertiliser"]}
  answer={1}
>
Most of a plant's dry mass comes from **carbon dioxide in the air**! Using light energy, leaves combine CO₂ and water to make glucose, the building material for the whole plant.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Photosynthesis" />

<Formula tex="\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \xrightarrow[\text{chlorophyll}]{\text{sunlight}} \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}" />

<ConceptCard title="The leaf factory" emoji="🏭">
- **Chlorophyll** in chloroplasts captures light energy.
- **Carbon dioxide** enters through tiny pores called **stomata**; **water** comes up from the roots.
- **Glucose** is used for energy and to build the plant, or stored as **starch**.
- **Oxygen** is released: the oxygen we breathe!
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Limiting factors" emoji="🚧">
Photosynthesis goes only as fast as its **scarcest** ingredient allows: light, carbon dioxide, water or a suitable temperature. That ingredient is the <KeyTerm def="The factor in shortest supply, which stops the rate of a process from going any faster.">limiting factor</KeyTerm>. Increase it and the rate goes up, until something else becomes limiting.
</ConceptCard>
