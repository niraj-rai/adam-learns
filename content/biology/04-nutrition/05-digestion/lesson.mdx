<Hook emoji="🍽️">
Your breakfast idli has a long trip ahead: about 8–9 metres through your body, taking a day or more. Along the way it is chewed, soaked in acid, squeezed, dissolved and absorbed, until what's left is a tiny fraction. How does a whole idli end up in your blood?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Where are most nutrients absorbed into the blood?"
  options={["Mouth", "Stomach", "Small intestine", "Large intestine"]}
  answer={2}
>
The **small intestine**: about 6–7 m long and lined with millions of tiny finger-like **villi** that give it a huge surface area.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="The digestive system" />

<ConceptCard title="Why digest?" emoji="🧩">
Food molecules like starch, proteins and fats are too big to pass into the blood. <KeyTerm def="The breaking down of large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble ones that can be absorbed.">Digestion</KeyTerm> breaks them into small, soluble pieces: starch → sugars, proteins → amino acids, fats → fatty acids and glycerol. Special chemicals called **enzymes** speed this up.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Digestion Journey" />

<Lab id="digestion-journey" />

<StepReveal>
<div>
**Mouth**: teeth chew; saliva (amylase) starts digesting starch.
</div>
<div>
**Oesophagus**: peristalsis pushes food to the stomach.
</div>
<div>
**Stomach**: acid kills germs; juices start digesting protein.
</div>
<div>
**Small intestine**: bile (from the liver) and pancreatic juice finish digestion; villi absorb nutrients.
</div>
<div>
**Large intestine**: water is absorbed; undigested fibre forms faeces, removed through the anus (**egestion**).
</div>
</StepReveal>

<RealLife title="Ruminants">
Cows and buffaloes quickly swallow grass and later bring it back up to chew again: **chewing the cud**. Their four-chambered stomach contains microbes that digest cellulose, which humans can't digest.
</RealLife>
