<Hook emoji="🗺️">
The Western Ghats are older than the Himalayas. They're home to lion-tailed macaques, purple frogs that live underground, Malabar giant squirrels and thousands of plants found nowhere else on Earth. But forests are being cleared for farms, roads, mines and towns. How can we protect this treasure while people still earn a living?
</Hook>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Biodiversity and its threats" />

<ConceptCard title="Biodiversity">
<KeyTerm def="The variety of all living things in an area">Biodiversity</KeyTerm> gives us food, medicines, clean water, clean air and fertile soil. India is one of 17 **megadiverse** countries, and the Western Ghats is a global **biodiversity hotspot**.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Protected areas in India">
**National parks** (like Bandipur and Nagarhole) strictly protect wildlife habitat. **Wildlife sanctuaries** protect animals but allow some human activities. **Biosphere reserves** (like the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, India's first, set up in 1986) protect large regions, including the people who live there. The **Red Data Book** lists endangered species.
</ConceptCard>
