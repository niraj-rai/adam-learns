<Hook emoji="🫘">
Soak some moong dal overnight, wrap it in a wet cloth, and in a day or two tiny white shoots appear: sprouts for a healthy breakfast! What woke those seeds up? And what if you kept them dry, in the fridge, or in the dark?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Do seeds need light to germinate?"
  options={["Yes, always", "No: they need water, air and warmth", "Only soil", "Only water"]}
  answer={1}
>
Seeds germinate using **food stored inside them**, so they don't need light or soil to start. They need **water**, **air** and a **suitable temperature**. Your moong sprouts grew in a dark cloth!
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Germination" />

<ConceptCard title="What a seed needs" emoji="💧">
<KeyTerm def="The process in which a seed starts to grow into a seedling.">Germination</KeyTerm> begins when a seed takes in **water**, swells and bursts its coat.
- **Water** activates the seed and lets the stored food be used.
- **Air (oxygen)** is needed for respiration, to release energy.
- **A suitable temperature**: too cold and too hot both stop it.
- Once the seedling has leaves, it needs **light** to make its own food.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Germination Lab" />

<Lab id="germination-lab" />

<ConceptCard title="Fair tests and controls" emoji="⚖️">
In a fair test, you change **only one** thing at a time. The **control** jar has everything the seeds might need; each other jar leaves out one thing. If only that jar fails, you know that factor was needed.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="Stop the breeding">
Because mosquito eggs, larvae and pupae all live in still water, emptying coolers, flower-pot trays, old tyres and buckets every week is one of the best ways to prevent dengue and malaria.
</RealLife>
