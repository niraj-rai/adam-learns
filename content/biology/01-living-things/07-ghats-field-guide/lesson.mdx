<Hook emoji="🔭">
The **Western Ghats** run for about 1600 km along India's west coast, from Gujarat to Kerala. They are one of the world's biodiversity hotspots and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, home to thousands of species of plants and animals, many found nowhere else on Earth. You've joined a research team. Your job: identify every organism you find.
</Hook>

<ConceptCard title="Your toolkit" emoji="🧰">
- Read the **field notes** carefully: they are your evidence.
- Answer each key question honestly, one step at a time.
- Remember: plants make their own food; vertebrates have backbones; feathers mean bird; fur means mammal; dry scales mean reptile; moist skin means amphibian; six legs and wings mean insect.
- If you reach the wrong name, retrace your steps.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="Protecting the Ghats">
Forests in the Western Ghats feed rivers like the Kaveri, Krishna and Godavari, which supply water to millions of people, including Bengaluru. Threats include deforestation, mining and new roads. Scientists, forest departments and local communities work together to protect species like the lion-tailed macaque.
</RealLife>

<Inquiry criterion="C" title="Field report">

</Inquiry>

<CbseCorner source="Class 6 Curiosity Ch 2">
Exam questions often give a short description of an organism and ask you to classify it or to explain an adaptation. Use the features you can see (legs, skin covering, how it breathes, how it feeds) as your evidence.
</CbseCorner>
