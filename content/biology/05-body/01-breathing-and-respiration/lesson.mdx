<Hook emoji="🫁">
You breathe about 20,000 times a day without thinking about it. Hold your breath and within a minute your body is begging for air. Why do we need oxygen so badly, and where does it actually go?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Is the air you breathe OUT the same as the air you breathe IN?"
  options={["Exactly the same", "It has more carbon dioxide and less oxygen", "It has no oxygen at all", "It has more oxygen"]}
  answer={1}
>
Breathed-out air still has plenty of oxygen (about 16%, compared with 21% in fresh air), which is why rescue breaths work. But it contains about **100 times more carbon dioxide**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Breathing" />

<ConceptCard title="How we breathe" emoji="🌬️">
- **Breathing in**: the **diaphragm** (a dome-shaped muscle under the lungs) flattens and moves down; the **ribs** move up and out. The chest gets bigger, pressure falls, and air rushes in.
- **Breathing out**: the diaphragm relaxes and moves up; the ribs move down and in. The chest gets smaller and air is pushed out.
- Air travels: nose → windpipe (trachea) → bronchi → millions of tiny air sacs (**alveoli**), where oxygen passes into the blood.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="breathing-lab" />

<ConceptCard title="Respiration: releasing energy" emoji="⚡">
**Respiration** happens inside every cell (in the mitochondria):

**glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy**

During very hard exercise, muscles can't get oxygen fast enough and respire **without oxygen**, making **lactic acid**, which causes cramps. Yeast also respires without oxygen, making the CO₂ that raises bread!
</ConceptCard>
