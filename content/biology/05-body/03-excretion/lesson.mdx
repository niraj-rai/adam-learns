<Hook emoji="🫘">
Every cell in your body makes waste as it works. If that waste built up, it would poison you. Two bean-shaped organs, each about the size of your fist, filter your entire blood supply dozens of times a day: your **kidneys**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="On a very hot day when you sweat a lot, what happens to your urine?"
  options={["You make more, paler urine", "You make less, darker urine", "Nothing changes", "You stop making urine"]}
  answer={1}
>
Your kidneys **save water** by reabsorbing more of it, so you make a little **concentrated** (darker) urine. Dark urine is a sign to drink more water.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Excretion" />

<ConceptCard title="What is excretion?" emoji="🚽">
<KeyTerm def="The removal of waste products made by the body's cells, such as urea and carbon dioxide.">Excretion</KeyTerm> removes wastes **made by cells**: the lungs excrete carbon dioxide, the skin excretes some water and salts in sweat, and the **kidneys** excrete **urea** (from broken-down proteins), extra water and salts as **urine**.
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal>
<div>
**1. Filtering**: blood is pushed through millions of tiny filters in the kidneys. Water, glucose, salts and urea pass through; cells and proteins stay in the blood.
</div>
<div>
**2. Taking back**: all the glucose and most of the water and salts are reabsorbed into the blood.
</div>
<div>
**3. Urine**: what's left (urea, extra water and salts) flows down the **ureters** to the **bladder**, and out through the **urethra**.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<RealLife title="When kidneys fail">
If the kidneys stop working, wastes build up in the blood. Patients need **dialysis**, where a machine filters their blood several times a week, or a **kidney transplant** from a donor. One healthy person can live well with just one kidney, so living relatives can donate.
</RealLife>
