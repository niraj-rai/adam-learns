<Hook emoji="🌈">
After a monsoon shower the Sun comes out behind you and a rainbow arcs across the sky. Stars twinkle, but planets don't. The sky is blue at noon and orange at sunset. All of these come from light bending and scattering in air and water.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst

  options={["It reflects the oceans", "Air scatters blue light much more than red", "Blue light is hotter", "The Sun gives out only blue light"]}
  answer={1}

Tiny air molecules scatter short wavelengths (blue) much more than long ones (red), so scattered blue light reaches your eyes from every direction.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Splitting and scattering light" />

<ConceptCard title="Dispersion" emoji="🔺">
White light is a mixture of colours. In glass, each colour travels at a slightly different speed, so each bends by a different amount: violet most, red least. A prism spreads white light into a <KeyTerm def="The band of colours (VIBGYOR) produced when white light is split by dispersion.">spectrum</KeyTerm>. A rainbow is dispersion plus internal reflection in millions of raindrops.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Atmospheric refraction" emoji="✨">
Air's refractive index changes with temperature, so starlight is bent by varying amounts as it comes through the atmosphere: stars (point sources) **twinkle**. The Sun appears about 2 minutes before actual sunrise and stays visible about 2 minutes after sunset.
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
C. V. Raman discovered that scattered light can change its wavelength slightly, the **Raman effect**, which revealed the structure of molecules. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930, and India celebrates National Science Day on 28 February to mark his discovery.
