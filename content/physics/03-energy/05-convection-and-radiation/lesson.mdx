<Stage kind="wonder" title="Heat on the move" />

<Hook emoji="☀️">
The Sun is 150 million km away, across empty space, yet you feel its warmth on your face. The water at the top of a pot on the stove gets hot, even though the flame is at the bottom. Neither can be conduction. So how does the heat get there?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A pot of water is heated from BELOW. Where does the hot water go?"
  options={["It stays at the bottom", "It rises to the top", "It moves to the sides", "It doesn't move"]}
  answer={1}
>
Hot water **expands**, becomes **less dense** and **rises**. Cooler, denser water sinks to take its place. This circulation is a **convection current**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Convection" />

<ConceptCard title="Convection: heat carried by moving fluids" emoji="🌀">
In <KeyTerm def="Heat transfer by the movement of a liquid or gas: warmer, less dense fluid rises and cooler, denser fluid sinks.">convection</KeyTerm>, the liquid or gas itself moves, carrying heat with it. It only happens in **fluids** (liquids and gases), because their particles can move around.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Currents and cans" />

<Lab id="convection-radiation" />

<RealLife title="Convection all around you">
- **Sea and land breezes** are giant convection currents (you met them in the Cyclone Lab in Unit 2).
- Air conditioners are fixed **high** on the wall: cool air sinks. Room heaters are placed **low**: warm air rises.
- Geyser heating coils sit near the **bottom** of the tank.
</RealLife>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Radiation" />

<ConceptCard title="Radiation: heat without a medium" emoji="☀️">
<KeyTerm def="Heat transfer by waves (infrared radiation) that can travel through empty space; no particles are needed.">Radiation</KeyTerm> doesn't need particles at all. It travels through **empty space**, which is how the Sun's energy reaches Earth. All warm objects give out radiation: a hot tawa, a fire, even you!
</ConceptCard>
