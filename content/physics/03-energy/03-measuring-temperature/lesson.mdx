<Hook emoji="🖐️">
Put one hand in cold water and the other in warm water for a minute. Then put both hands in a bowl of lukewarm water. One hand says "warm!", the other says "cold!" Same water, two answers. That's why scientists use **thermometers**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst

A clinical thermometer is designed for the human body: **35 °C to 42 °C**. Put it in boiling water and it could crack. For liquids in the lab, use a **laboratory thermometer** (about −10 °C to 110 °C).
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Mercury is being phased out" emoji="⚠️">
Older thermometers contained **mercury**, which is toxic if the glass breaks. Hospitals and homes are switching to **digital thermometers** and alcohol-filled thermometers, which are safer.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Three temperature scales" emoji="🔁">
- **Celsius (°C)**: water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C.
- **Fahrenheit (°F)**: water freezes at 32 °F and boils at 212 °F. Still used for fever in many Indian homes: normal is about 98.6 °F.
- **Kelvin (K)**: the SI unit. 0 K is **absolute zero**, the coldest possible temperature.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="{}^\circ\text{F} = {}^\circ\text{C} \times \tfrac{9}{5} + 32 \qquad \text{K} = {}^\circ\text{C} + 273" />

<StepReveal>
<div>
**Worked example:** Delhi reaches 45 °C in May. What is this in °F and K?
</div>
<div>
°F = 45 × 9/5 + 32 = 81 + 32 = **113 °F**
</div>
<div>
K = 45 + 273 = **318 K**
</div>
</StepReveal>
