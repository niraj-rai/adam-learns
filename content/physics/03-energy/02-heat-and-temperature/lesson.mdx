<Hook emoji="🪣">
A cup of steaming chai is hotter than a bucket of warm bath water. But which could melt more ice? Most people guess the chai... and most people are wrong! To see why, we need to separate two ideas that sound alike: **heat** and **temperature**.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="You mix 200 g of water at 80 °C with 200 g of water at 20 °C. What is the final temperature?"
  options={["100 °C", "60 °C", "50 °C", "20 °C"]}
  answer={2}
>
Heat flows from the hot water to the cold water until they're at the same temperature. Equal amounts meet exactly **halfway: 50 °C**. Temperatures don't add up!
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Heat flows from hot to cold" emoji="➡️">
Put a hot object next to a cold one and heat flows from hot to cold. The hot one cools, the cold one warms, until they reach the same temperature: <KeyTerm def="The state reached when objects in contact are at the same temperature, so there is no more net flow of heat.">thermal equilibrium</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="When you touch ice, cold flows into your hand.">
There's no such thing as "cold" flowing. **Heat** flows out of your warm hand into the ice. Your hand loses energy, so it feels cold.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
The temperature of the sparks from a sparkler (phuljhadi) on Diwali can be over 1000 °C, yet they rarely burn you. Each spark is so tiny that it holds very little heat energy!
</DidYouKnow>
