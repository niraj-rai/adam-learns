<Stage kind="wonder" title="Who pushes whom?" />

<Hook emoji="⛸️">
Two skaters stand still on ice and push each other. **Both** glide backwards, even the one who "did nothing". You can't push something without it pushing you back. Newton's third law explains walking, swimming, rockets and the kick of a water gun.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A big skater (80 kg) and a small skater (40 kg) push each other apart. Which feels the bigger force?"
  options={["The big skater", "The small skater", "Both feel the same size force", "Only the one who pushes"]}
  answer={2}
>
The forces are **equal in size and opposite in direction**. The small skater just accelerates more, because a = F ÷ m.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Forces come in pairs" />

<ConceptCard title="Newton's third law" emoji="🤝">
When object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts a force on object A that is:
- **equal** in size,
- **opposite** in direction,
- of the **same type** (both contact, both gravitational…),
- acting on the **other** object.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Why don't they cancel?" emoji="❓">
Forces only cancel when they act on the **same** object. An action-reaction pair acts on **two different** objects, so each object feels one of the forces, and each can accelerate. The book on a table is different: its weight and the table's push both act on the book. They balance, but they are **not** a third-law pair.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Action and Reaction" />

<Lab id="action-reaction" />

<MythFact myth="A rocket pushes against the air to take off.">
Rockets work best in space, where there's no air at all. The engine pushes exhaust gas backwards at huge speed, and the gas pushes the rocket forwards. ISRO's PSLV and the Chandrayaan missions all rely on this reaction force.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
When you jump, you push the whole Earth away from you with the same force it pushes you up. The Earth does move, but its mass is about 10²³ times yours, so its acceleration is far too tiny to notice.
</DidYouKnow>
