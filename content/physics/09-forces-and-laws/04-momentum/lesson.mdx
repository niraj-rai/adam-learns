<Hook emoji="🏏">
Watch a fielder catch a fast cricket ball: their hands move backwards with the ball as they catch it. Nobody taught them the physics, but it works. A moving object carries **momentum**, and how quickly you take it away decides how much it hurts.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Which has more momentum: a 0.16 kg cricket ball at 40 m/s, or a 60 kg person walking at 1 m/s?"
  options={["The cricket ball", "The walking person", "They are equal", "You can't compare them"]}
  answer={1}
>
Ball: 0.16 × 40 = **6.4 kg m/s**. Person: 60 × 1 = **60 kg m/s**. Momentum depends on mass *and* velocity.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Momentum" />

<Formula tex="p = m \times v" caption="p = momentum (kg m/s), m = mass (kg), v = velocity (m/s). Momentum has a direction, like velocity." />

<ConceptCard title="Conservation of momentum" emoji="🔒">
When objects collide or push apart, and no outside force acts, the <KeyTerm def="In a collision or explosion with no outside forces, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after.">total momentum is conserved</KeyTerm>:

**total momentum before = total momentum after**

This follows from the third law: the forces on the two objects are equal and opposite and last for the same time, so one gains exactly the momentum the other loses.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="collision-lab" />

<ConceptCard title="Force and change of momentum" emoji="🎈">
Newton actually wrote his second law as: force = rate of change of momentum.

**F = (mv − mu) ÷ t**

To stop the same momentum, a **longer time** needs a **smaller force**. That's why fielders pull their hands back, why gymnasts land on soft mats, and why cars have airbags, seat belts that stretch slightly, and <KeyTerm def="Parts at the front and back of a car designed to crumple slowly in a crash, making the stopping time longer and the force smaller.">crumple zones</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="When two objects stick together, momentum is lost.">
Momentum is **never** lost in a collision (without outside forces). Kinetic **energy** is partly changed to heat, sound and bending, but the total momentum is exactly the same before and after.
</MythFact>

<RealLife>
India made airbags for the driver and front passenger compulsory in new cars from 2022, and crash-test safety ratings (Bharat NCAP) started in 2023. Every one of these features works by making the time of a collision longer.
</RealLife>
