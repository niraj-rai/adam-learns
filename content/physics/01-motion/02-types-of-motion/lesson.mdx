To someone on the platform, Adam is **moving** with the train. But to Adam's friend sitting next to him, Adam is **at rest**! Whether something is moving depends on the **reference point** you compare it with.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Describing motion" />

<ConceptCard title="Motion and reference points" emoji="📍">
An object is **in motion** if its position changes compared with a **reference point** over time. If its position doesn't change, it is **at rest** relative to that point.

On a map, "the school is 2 km north of the bus stand" uses the bus stand as a reference point.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="motion-sorter" />

<ConceptCard title="Periodic motion" emoji="🔁">
Motion that **repeats itself after a fixed time** is called **periodic motion**. A swing, a pendulum, the hands of a clock, and the Earth going around the Sun are all periodic. Periodic motion is so regular that we use it to **measure time**, as you'll see in Topic 1.5!
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Circular motion and oscillatory motion are the same thing.">
In **circular** motion, the object keeps going around in the **same direction**. In **oscillatory** motion, it goes **back and forth**, reversing its direction each time. A fan blade goes round; a swing goes to and fro.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
Many things show **more than one** type of motion at once! A bicycle wheel **rotates** (circular) while the whole bicycle moves in a **straight line**. A rolling cricket ball does the same.
</DidYouKnow>
