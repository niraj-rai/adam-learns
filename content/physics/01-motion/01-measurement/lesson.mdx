<Stage kind="explain" title="Standard units" />

<ConceptCard title="From body parts to the metre" emoji="🌏">
Long ago in India, people measured with the **angula** (finger width), **mutthi** (fist), **vitasti** (handspan) and **hasta** (elbow to fingertip, a "cubit"). The Harappan civilisation even had remarkably precise measuring scales!

Today, scientists worldwide use the **International System of Units (SI)**. The SI unit of length is the **metre (m)**.
</ConceptCard>

| Unit | Symbol | How big? |
|---|---|---|
| millimetre | mm | 10 mm = 1 cm (the smallest marks on your ruler) |
| centimetre | cm | 100 cm = 1 m |
| metre | m | about one big step |
| kilometre | km | 1 km = 1000 m (about a 12-minute walk) |

<ConceptCard title="Measuring correctly" emoji="🎯">
- **Place the object along the scale**, touching it.
- **Zero error:** if the ruler's end is broken, start at a clear mark (say 1 cm), then **subtract** that starting value from the end reading.
- **Parallax error:** keep your eye **directly above** the mark. Looking from the side gives a wrong reading.
- For **curved lines**, lay a thread along the curve, mark it, then straighten it along the ruler.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="You must always start measuring from the end of the ruler.">
Many rulers have a worn or broken end, and the first mark may not be exactly at the edge. Start from any clear mark and subtract. What matters is the **difference** between the two readings.
</MythFact>

<DidYouKnow>
The metre was once defined as one ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the Equator! Today it's defined using the speed of light: the distance light travels in 1/299,792,458 of a second.
</DidYouKnow>
