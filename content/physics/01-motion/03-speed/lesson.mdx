<Hook emoji="🏃">
Adam runs 100 m in 20 seconds. His cousin Riya cycles 600 m in 2 minutes. Riya went much further, but she also took much longer. How can we fairly compare who was faster?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Adam: 100 m in 20 s. Riya: 600 m in 120 s. Who is faster?"
  options={["Adam", "Riya", "They have the same speed", "Can't tell"]}
  answer={2}
>
Work out the **distance covered each second**: Adam 100 ÷ 20 = **5 m/s**; Riya 600 ÷ 120 = **5 m/s**. The same speed! Comparing distance alone, or time alone, can fool you.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Average speed" emoji="📊">
Most real journeys are non-uniform. **Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.**

Example: a bus travels 150 km from Bengaluru to Mysuru in 3 hours (with traffic and stops). Average speed = 150 ÷ 3 = **50 km/h**, even though it sometimes went 80 km/h and sometimes stood still.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Average speed is the average of the speeds.">
If you go 60 km/h for 1 hour, then 20 km/h for 3 hours, your average isn't (60 + 20) ÷ 2 = 40 km/h. It's total distance ÷ total time = (60 + 60) ÷ 4 = **30 km/h**, because you spent longer going slowly.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Speedometer and odometer">
A vehicle's **speedometer** shows its speed at that instant (in km/h). The **odometer** shows the total distance it has travelled (in km). Next time you're in a car or auto, check both!
</RealLife>
