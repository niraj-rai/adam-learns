<Hook emoji="🌏">
In 1990, the Voyager 1 space probe turned its camera back towards home from about 6 billion km away. Earth appeared as a tiny pale blue dot, less than a pixel wide. Of all the planets we know, it's the only one we know of with liquid water on its surface, a breathable atmosphere and life. What makes it so special?
</Hook>

<Stage kind="explain" title="A life-supporting planet" />

<ConceptCard title="What makes Earth habitable" emoji="🌱">
- **The right distance** from the Sun: in the <KeyTerm def="The range of distances from a star where a planet could have liquid water on its surface: not too hot, not too cold.">Goldilocks zone</KeyTerm>, where water can be liquid.
- **An atmosphere** with oxygen to breathe and a natural **greenhouse effect** that keeps it warm.
- **Liquid water**, which covers about 71% of the surface.
- **A magnetic field** and an **ozone layer** that shield life from harmful particles and ultraviolet radiation.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Protecting our home" emoji="🛡️">
Burning coal, petrol and diesel adds extra carbon dioxide and strengthens the greenhouse effect, warming the planet: more heatwaves, changing monsoons and melting Himalayan glaciers. CFCs once damaged the ozone layer until the world agreed to ban them in the **Montreal Protocol** (1987), a success story showing that global action works.
</ConceptCard>
