On Karva Chauth and Eid, people wait eagerly for the Moon. On Diwali night, there's no Moon at all, which is why the lamps look so bright. On Guru Purnima and Holi, it's big and round. The Moon doesn't really change shape, so why does it look so different through the month?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="What causes the phases of the Moon?"
  options={["Earth's shadow falling on the Moon", "How much of the Moon's sunlit half we can see", "Clouds covering part of the Moon", "The Moon changing size"]}
  answer={1}
>
The Sun **always lights half** of the Moon. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of that lit half. Earth's shadow only causes **eclipses**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Phases" />

<ConceptCard title="A month of phases" emoji="🌑🌓🌕🌗">
The Moon orbits Earth in about **29.5 days**:
- **Amavasya (new moon)**: the Moon is between Earth and the Sun; its lit side faces away, so we see nothing.
- **Waxing (Shukla paksha)**: crescent → first quarter → gibbous, as the lit part grows.
- **Purnima (full moon)**: Earth is between the Sun and the Moon; we see the whole lit face.
- **Waning (Krishna paksha)**: gibbous → last quarter → crescent, back to Amavasya.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="There should be an eclipse every new moon and full moon.">
The Moon's orbit is tilted by about **5°** compared with Earth's. At most new and full moons, the Moon passes just above or below the Sun–Earth line. Eclipses happen only when a new or full moon falls near a **node**, where the two orbits cross.
</MythFact>
