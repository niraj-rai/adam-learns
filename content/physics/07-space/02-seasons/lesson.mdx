**Winter!** So distance can't be the cause of the seasons. The real reason is Earth's **tilt**.

<Stage kind="explain" title="The tilted Earth" />

<ConceptCard title="Tilt, not distance" emoji="🌍">
Earth's axis is tilted by about **23.5°** and always points the same way in space as Earth **revolves** around the Sun once a year.
- Around **June**, the northern hemisphere leans **towards** the Sun: the Sun climbs higher, sunlight hits the ground more directly, and days are longer. That's **summer** in India.
- Around **December**, the northern hemisphere leans **away**: the Sun stays low and days are short. That's **winter**, while the southern hemisphere has summer.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Solstices and equinoxes" emoji="📆">
- **Summer solstice** (about 21 June): the longest day in the northern hemisphere.
- **Winter solstice** (about 21 December): the shortest day.
- **Equinoxes** (about 20 March and 22 September): day and night are nearly equal everywhere.

- Earth **revolves** around the Sun in about 365¼ days, with its axis tilted.
- The tilt causes **seasons** and changes in the length of day and night.
- When it's summer in the northern hemisphere, it's winter in the southern hemisphere.
