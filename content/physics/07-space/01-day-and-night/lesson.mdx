<Stage kind="wonder" title="Who moves: the Sun or us?" />

<Hook emoji="🌅">
Every morning the Sun rises in the east, climbs across the sky and sets in the west. For thousands of years, most people believed the Sun travelled around the Earth. It certainly looks that way! But over 1500 years ago, the Indian astronomer **Aryabhata** suggested that it's the Earth that turns. Who was right?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Why does the Sun appear to move across the sky each day?"
  options={["The Sun orbits the Earth every day", "The Earth spins on its axis", "The Moon pushes the Sun", "The clouds carry it"]}
  answer={1}
>
The **Earth rotates** on its axis once every 24 hours. Aryabhata compared it to a boat: someone on a moving boat sees the trees on the bank moving backwards.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Rotation" />

<ConceptCard title="Day and night" emoji="🌗">
Earth spins on an imaginary line through the North and South Poles, its <KeyTerm def="The imaginary line through the North and South Poles around which the Earth spins.">axis</KeyTerm>. One full spin, a **rotation**, takes about 24 hours. At any moment, the half facing the Sun has **day** and the other half has **night**. Earth spins from **west to east**, so the Sun appears to rise in the east and set in the west.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="day-night" />

<ConceptCard title="Shadow clocks" emoji="🕰️">
A stick's shadow points away from the Sun: **west in the morning**, **east in the afternoon**. It is **longest** when the Sun is low and **shortest at noon**. A **sundial** uses this to tell the time. The giant sundials of Jaipur's **Jantar Mantar**, built in the 1700s, can tell the time to within a few seconds!
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
Bengaluru and other places between the Tropics get a **Zero Shadow Day** twice a year, when the noon Sun is directly overhead and upright objects cast no shadow. For Bengaluru, it falls in late April and mid-August.
</DidYouKnow>
