<Hook emoji="🌬️">
On a hot afternoon at the beach, a cool breeze blows in from the sea. Every June the monsoon winds bring rain to Kerala, then the rest of India. And sometimes, over the Bay of Bengal, the winds wind up into a giant spinning cyclone. What drives all this moving air?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Air pressure is HIGH over the sea and LOW over the land. Which way does the wind blow?"
  options={["From land to sea", "From sea to land", "Straight up", "No wind"]}
  answer={1}
>
Air always moves **from high pressure to low pressure**, so it blows from sea to land. That moving air is **wind**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Uneven heating makes wind" />

<ConceptCard title="Hot air rises" emoji="🔥">
When air is heated, it **expands** and becomes less dense, so it **rises**. This leaves **lower pressure** near the ground. Cooler air from nearby, at **higher pressure**, moves in to take its place. That's wind!
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="The monsoon: a giant sea breeze">
In summer the Indian landmass gets far hotter than the Indian Ocean. Low pressure forms over the land, and moist winds blow in from the ocean, bringing the **southwest monsoon** from June to September. About three-quarters of India's yearly rain comes this way.
</RealLife>

<ConceptCard title="How a cyclone forms" emoji="🌀">
1. The Sun heats the ocean above about **26–27 °C**.
2. Warm, moist air rises fast, leaving **very low pressure** at the surface.
3. Air rushes in from all sides and begins to **spiral**: anticlockwise in the northern hemisphere.
4. As the rising air cools, water vapour condenses into clouds, releasing heat that powers the storm even more.
5. A calm **eye** forms at the centre, surrounded by the eyewall with the fiercest winds.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Why cyclones are dangerous" emoji="⚠️">
- **Violent winds** (over 200 km/h in the strongest storms) tear off roofs and bring down trees.
- **Heavy rain** causes floods.
- A **storm surge**, a wall of sea water pushed onto the coast, is often the deadliest part.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="Odisha: from tragedy to world model">
The 1999 super cyclone killed about 10,000 people in Odisha. The state then built hundreds of cyclone shelters and trained volunteers. When the very severe Cyclone Phailin struck in 2013, IMD warnings and the evacuation of about a million people kept the death toll below 50.
</RealLife>
