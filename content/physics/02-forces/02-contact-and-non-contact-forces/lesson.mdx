<Hook emoji="🎈">
Rub a balloon on your hair, and it can lift tiny bits of paper **without touching them**. A magnet can drag a paperclip across a table from a distance. A mango falls from a tree with nothing pulling it... or is there?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="You bring the SOUTH pole of one magnet close to the SOUTH pole of another. What happens?"
  options={["They attract", "They repel", "Nothing", "They swap poles"]}
  answer={1}

**Like poles repel**; unlike poles (N and S) attract. You can feel the push even before the magnets touch. It's a **non-contact force**.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Magnetic force" emoji="🧲">
- Magnets attract **iron, nickel, cobalt** and **steel**. They don't attract most metals (aluminium, copper, gold).
- Every magnet has a **north** and a **south** pole. **Like poles repel, unlike poles attract.**
- The force gets **weaker** as the gap increases.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Electrostatic force" emoji="⚡">
Rubbing can move tiny charged particles (electrons) from one material to another. The object becomes **charged** and can attract light things like paper bits, hair or dust. This is an <KeyTerm def="The force between electrically charged objects. Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.">electrostatic force</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Gravitational force" emoji="🌍">
**Every** object with mass attracts every other object. It's tiny between two people, but the Earth is so massive that its pull keeps us on the ground and the Moon in orbit.
</ConceptCard>
