<Hook emoji="🛺">
An autorickshaw has broken down on a Bengaluru road. One person pushes, and nothing happens. Two push, and it starts to creep forward. Then a friend "helps" by pushing from the front! What is really going on between all those pushes?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Two teams push a heavy box from opposite sides with exactly the same force. What happens?"
  options={["It moves towards the stronger-looking team", "It doesn't move", "It moves slowly", "It spins"]}
  answer={1}
>
Equal forces in opposite directions **cancel out**. The **net force** is zero, so the box stays put. These forces are **balanced**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="What is a force?" />

<ConceptCard title="A force is a push or a pull" emoji="💪">
- A force acts when two objects **interact**: your hand and a door, the Earth and a mango.
- A force has a **size (magnitude)** and a **direction**, so we draw it as an **arrow**.
- Force is measured in <KeyTerm def="The SI unit of force. About the weight of a small apple (100 g) on Earth.">newtons (N)</KeyTerm>, using a **spring balance** (newton meter).
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="What can a force do?" emoji="🔄">
A force can:
1. make a still object **move**
2. **stop** a moving object
3. make an object **speed up** or **slow down**
4. change its **direction**
5. change its **shape**: squash a ball of dough, stretch a rubber band
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Net force" emoji="➕">
- Forces in the **same direction** add: 200 N + 150 N = **350 N**.
- Forces in **opposite directions** subtract: 500 N − 420 N = **80 N**, in the direction of the bigger force.
- The total is called the <KeyTerm def="The overall (resultant) force on an object when all the forces acting on it are combined.">net force</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="A moving object needs a force to keep it moving.">
In space, a probe keeps coasting for years with its engines off! On Earth things seem to "need" a push only because **friction** and **air resistance** keep slowing them down. With balanced forces, a moving object just keeps going.
</MythFact>

<QuickCheck question="A cyclist pedals with a forward force of 80 N. Friction and air resistance together are 80 N. What happens?" options={["She speeds up", "She slows down", "She keeps moving at a steady speed", "She stops at once"]} answer={2}>
The forces are **balanced** (net force = 0), so her speed doesn't change. She moves at a **steady speed**.
</QuickCheck>

<CbseCorner source="Class 8 Curiosity Ch 5">
- Force = **push or pull**; it arises from an **interaction** between objects.
- Effects of force: change in **speed, direction of motion or shape**.
- Forces in the same direction add; forces in opposite directions subtract. The **SI unit is the newton (N)**.
</CbseCorner>
