The deeper you go, the more water there is above pushing down, so **pressure increases with depth**. The bottom hole squirts furthest.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Pressure in liquids" />

<ConceptCard title="Liquid pressure" emoji="💧">
- Liquids exert pressure on the **bottom and walls** of their container, in **all directions**.
- Pressure **increases with depth**.
- At the same depth, a **denser** liquid (like honey) exerts more pressure than a less dense one (like oil).
- The **shape** of the container doesn't matter, only the depth.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="liquid-pressure" />

<ConceptCard title="Atmospheric pressure" emoji="🌬️">
We live at the bottom of an ocean of air about 100 km deep. Its weight presses on everything with about **10 N on every cm²**. That's like a 1 kg bag on every fingernail! We don't feel crushed because the air inside our bodies pushes back equally.
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal>
<div>
**How does a straw work?** When you "suck", you lower the air pressure inside the straw.
</div>
<div>
The **atmospheric pressure** on the surface of the juice is now bigger than the pressure in the straw.
</div>
<div>
So the air **pushes** the juice up the straw. You don't pull it; the air pushes it!
</div>
</StepReveal>

<MythFact myth="A suction hook sticks because it sucks onto the wall.">
Pressing the hook squeezes the air out from behind it. The **air pressure outside** is now much larger than inside, and it **pushes** the hook against the wall.
</MythFact>

<RealLife title="Deep divers and submarines">
Every 10 m of water adds about as much pressure as the whole atmosphere. Deep-sea submarines like India's planned **Matsya-6000** (for the Samudrayaan mission) need thick, spherical hulls to survive pressures hundreds of times atmospheric pressure.
</RealLife>
