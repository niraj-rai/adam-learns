Glass is the smoothest, so it has the **least friction** and the block starts sliding first. Rough sandpaper grips the block until the ramp is very steep.

<Stage kind="explain" title="What is friction?" />

<ConceptCard title="Friction opposes motion" emoji="✋">
<KeyTerm def="A contact force between two surfaces that opposes their motion (or attempted motion) relative to each other.">Friction</KeyTerm> acts wherever two surfaces touch. Even surfaces that look smooth have tiny bumps (irregularities) that catch and lock together.

- It acts **opposite** to the motion, or to the direction something is being pushed.
- **Static friction** stops things from starting to move. **Sliding friction** acts once they move.
- Friction produces **heat**. Rub your hands together!
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Friction Ramp" />

<Lab id="friction-ramp" />

<ConceptCard title="What affects friction?" emoji="🔍">
- **Roughness of the surfaces**: rougher means more friction.
- **How hard the surfaces are pressed together**: a heavier box has more friction.
- **Rolling vs sliding**: rolling friction is much smaller. That's why we invented **wheels** and **ball bearings**.
- In air and water, friction is called <KeyTerm def="The friction force from a fluid (air or water) on a moving object. Streamlined shapes reduce it.">drag</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

**How we INCREASE friction:** treads on tyres and shoes, sand on icy roads, chalk powder on gymnasts' hands, rubber grips on bats.
</div>
<div>
**How we REDUCE friction:** oil and grease (lubricants), wheels and ball bearings, polishing, talcum powder on a carrom board, streamlined shapes.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<MythFact myth="Friction only acts when something is moving.">
Push a heavy almirah gently and it doesn't budge. **Static friction** is pushing back exactly as hard as you push! Only when your push beats the maximum static friction does the almirah start to slide.
</MythFact>

<CbseCorner source="Class 8 Curiosity Ch 5">
- Friction **opposes relative motion** between surfaces; it depends on the **nature of the surfaces**.
- Friction can be **increased** (treads, grooves) or **reduced** (lubricants, wheels, ball bearings, polishing).
- Rolling friction is less than sliding friction. Fluid friction (**drag**) is reduced by streamlined shapes.
</CbseCorner>
