<Hook emoji="🥣">
The **jal tarang** is a row of porcelain bowls filled with water. A musician strikes them with thin sticks and plays whole ragas. Change the water and you change the note. How can water turn a bowl into an instrument?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="You add more water to a jal tarang bowl and strike it. What happens to the pitch?"
  options={["It goes up", "It goes down", "It stays the same", "The bowl stops making a sound"]}
  answer={1}
>
The pitch goes **down**. The bowl and water together are heavier, so they vibrate more slowly when struck.
</PredictFirst>

<Lab id="music-lab" />

<ConceptCard title="What vibrates matters" emoji="🍾">
Tap a bottle and the **glass and water** vibrate: more water, lower pitch. Blow across it and the **air column** vibrates: more water leaves a shorter air column, so the pitch goes **higher**. Always ask: *what* is vibrating?
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
The **Saraswati veena** has 24 frets and a large wooden resonator. The resonator vibrates along with the strings, making the sound much louder and richer. Most string instruments have a hollow body for the same reason.
</DidYouKnow>
