<Hook emoji="🚢">
Light can't reach far into the deep ocean, so how do ships know how deep the water is, or find a shipwreck on the seabed? They listen! A ship sends a pulse of sound down and times the echo. Bats and dolphins have used the same trick for millions of years.
</Hook>

<ConceptCard title="Your toolkit" emoji="🧰">
- Sound travels at about **1500 m/s** in sea water.
- An echo goes **down and back up**, so **depth = speed × time ÷ 2**.
- **SONAR** = Sound Navigation And Ranging. It usually uses **ultrasound**, which makes a narrow beam.
- A sudden change in depth on a smooth seabed can reveal rocks, reefs or wrecks.
</ConceptCard>

**SONAR** uses ultrasound echoes to measure the depth of the sea and to find underwater objects. A typical exam question: “A sonar signal returns after t seconds. Find the depth.” Always remember to **divide by 2**.
