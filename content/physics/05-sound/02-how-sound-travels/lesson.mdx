<Stage kind="explain" title="Sound needs a medium" />

<ConceptCard title="Passing on the vibration" emoji="🔁">
Sound travels as a chain reaction: vibrating particles push their neighbours, which push theirs, and so on. It needs a <KeyTerm def="A material (solid, liquid or gas) through which sound can travel.">medium</KeyTerm>. The particles themselves only jiggle to and fro. What travels is the pattern of **compressions** (squashed-together regions) and **rarefactions** (spread-out regions).
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Echoes" emoji="🏔️">
An **echo** is sound reflected from a surface like a cliff or a big wall. Because the sound goes there **and back**, the distance to the surface is:

**distance = speed × time ÷ 2**

You hear a separate echo only if it returns at least 0.1 s later, so you must be at least about 17 m from the wall.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="Counting thunder">
Light travels almost instantly, but sound takes about 3 seconds to travel 1 km. Count the seconds between a lightning flash and the thunder, and divide by 3 to estimate how many kilometres away the storm is.
</RealLife>
