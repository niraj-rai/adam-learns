<Stage kind="explain" title="How we hear" />

<StepReveal>
<div>
**1. The outer ear** collects sound waves and funnels them into the ear canal.
</div>
<div>
**2. The eardrum**, a thin stretched membrane, vibrates when sound hits it.
</div>
<div>
**3. Three tiny bones** (the hammer, anvil and stirrup, the smallest bones in your body) pass on and amplify the vibrations.
</div>
<div>
**4. The cochlea**, a snail-shaped tube full of fluid, has thousands of hair cells that turn vibrations into nerve signals.
</div>
<div>
**5. The brain** interprets the signals as sound.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<Lab id="noise-lab" />

<ConceptCard title="Noise pollution" emoji="🚨">
<KeyTerm def="Unwanted or excessive sound that can harm health and wellbeing.">Noise pollution</KeyTerm> causes stress, poor sleep, high blood pressure and hearing loss. Sources in Indian cities include traffic and honking, construction, loudspeakers and firecrackers.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Reducing noise" emoji="🌳">
- **At the source**: less honking, silencers on vehicles, quieter machines, time limits for loudspeakers.
- **On the way**: trees and hedges, sound barriers along flyovers, double-glazed windows.
- **At the listener**: earplugs or earmuffs in noisy workplaces, and keeping earphone volume below 60%.
</ConceptCard>
