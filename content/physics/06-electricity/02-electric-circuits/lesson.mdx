<Hook emoji="🔦">
You have a cell, a torch bulb and one piece of wire. You touch the wire from the cell's + end to the bulb... nothing. Twist it, press harder... still nothing. What is missing?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="What do you need to light the bulb with the cell?"
  options={["A thicker wire", "A second wire, to make a complete loop back to the other terminal", "A bigger bulb", "Nothing more: it should work"]}
  answer={1}
>
Current needs a **complete, closed loop**: out of one terminal of the cell, through the bulb, and back into the other terminal. One wire is not enough.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Complete circuits" />

<ConceptCard title="The parts of a circuit" emoji="🔋">
- A **cell** pushes the current. It has a **positive (+)** and a **negative (−)** terminal. Two or more cells joined + to − make a **battery**.
- **Wires** connect the parts.
- A **switch** opens (off) or closes (on) the circuit.
- A **bulb** or **LED** lights up when current flows. An <KeyTerm def="Light Emitting Diode: a device that lights up when current flows through it in one direction only. Its longer leg goes towards the + terminal.">LED</KeyTerm> works only one way round.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Circuit diagrams" emoji="✏️">
Scientists draw circuits with standard **symbols**: a cell is a long thin line (+) and a short thick line (−); a bulb is a circle with a cross; a switch is a gap with a lever. The Circuit Builder uses these symbols.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="The bulb uses up the current.">
The current is the same all the way round a series circuit: what goes out of the cell comes back in. The bulb changes electrical **energy** into light and heat; the current isn't used up.
</MythFact>

- An electric **cell** has + and − terminals; a **battery** is a combination of cells.
- A **closed (complete) circuit** is needed for current to flow; a **switch** opens and closes it.
- **Circuit diagrams** use symbols; an **LED** glows only when connected the right way round.
