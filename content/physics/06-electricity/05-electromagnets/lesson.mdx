<Hook emoji="🏗️">
At a scrapyard, a crane swings a huge disc over a pile of old cars. It lifts a whole car into the air... then drops it exactly where the operator wants. A normal magnet couldn't let go. How does this one?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A compass is placed under a wire. What happens when current flows through the wire?"
  options={["Nothing", "The needle moves", "The compass gets hot", "The wire becomes a permanent magnet"]}
  answer={1}
>
The needle **moves**! A wire carrying a current has a **magnetic field** around it. Hans Christian Ørsted discovered this in 1820.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Electricity makes magnetism" />

<ConceptCard title="The magnetic effect of current" emoji="⚡🧲">
Every current produces a magnetic field. Coil the wire into many **turns** and the fields add together. Put an **iron core** inside the coil and the field becomes much stronger. You have an <KeyTerm def="A coil of wire, usually around an iron core, that becomes a magnet only while current flows through it.">electromagnet</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="Electromagnets all around you">
The electric motor in a ceiling fan or a mixer grinder uses electromagnets to spin. Speakers use one to vibrate a cone. Doorbells, MRI scanners in hospitals, and maglev trains that float above the track all depend on electromagnets.
</RealLife>
