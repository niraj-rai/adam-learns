<Hook emoji="⚙️">
The ceiling fan above you, the mixer in the kitchen and India's electric scooters and metro trains all rely on one effect: a wire carrying a current in a magnetic field is **pushed**. Turn that push into spinning and you have a motor.
</Hook>

Reversing either the current or the field **reverses the force**.

<Stage kind="explain" title="Force, motors and induction" />

<ConceptCard title="Fleming's left-hand rule" emoji="🤚">
Stretch the thumb, first finger and second finger of your **left** hand at right angles. **F**irst finger: **F**ield (N to S). Se**c**ond finger: **C**urrent. Thu**m**b: **M**otion (force). The force is biggest when the current is at right angles to the field.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Electromagnetic induction" emoji="🔁">
Michael Faraday found the reverse effect (1831): moving a wire through a magnetic field, or changing the field through a coil, **induces** a current. Generators in power stations, including hydro plants on India's rivers, spin coils in magnetic fields to make the electricity that reaches your home.
</ConceptCard>
