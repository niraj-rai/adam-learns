Add a second bulb in series and both get dimmer. Add it in parallel and both stay bright, but the battery runs down faster. The way resistors are connected changes everything.

In parallel, the current has two paths, so it's easier to flow: 1/R = 1/10 + 1/10, **R = 5 Ω**.

<Stage kind="explain" title="What affects resistance" />

<Formula tex="R = \rho \frac{L}{A}" caption="ρ = resistivity (Ω m), L = length, A = cross-sectional area" />

<ConceptCard title="Resistivity" emoji="🧵">
Resistance increases with **length**, decreases with **thickness**, and depends on the material through its <KeyTerm def="A property of a material that measures how strongly it resists current: R = ρL/A. Unit: ohm metre (Ω m).">resistivity</KeyTerm> ρ. Copper and aluminium (low ρ) make good wires; nichrome (high ρ) makes heating elements; tungsten makes filaments.
</ConceptCard>
