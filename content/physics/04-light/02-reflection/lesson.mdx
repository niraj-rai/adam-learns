<Hook emoji="🔦">
Shine a torch at a mirror and a bright spot appears on the opposite wall. Move the torch and the spot moves too, always to a very particular place. Is there a rule that tells us exactly where the light will go?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A light ray hits a mirror at 30° from the normal. At what angle does it bounce off?"
  options={["60°", "30°", "90°", "0°"]}
  answer={1}
>
**30°.** The angle of reflection always equals the angle of incidence. This is the **law of reflection**.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="The law of reflection" />

<ConceptCard title="Words we need" emoji="📐">
- **Incident ray**: the ray coming in towards the mirror.
- **Reflected ray**: the ray bouncing off.
- <KeyTerm def="An imaginary line drawn at 90° to the mirror where the ray hits it. All angles are measured from it.">Normal</KeyTerm>: a line at 90° to the mirror at the point where the ray hits.
- **Angle of incidence (∠i)** and **angle of reflection (∠r)** are measured **from the normal**, not from the mirror.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="\angle i = \angle r" caption="The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane" />

<MythFact myth="Rough surfaces don't obey the law of reflection.">
They do! Each tiny part of a rough surface obeys ∠i = ∠r. But the tiny parts face in different directions, so the reflected rays scatter everywhere.
</MythFact>
