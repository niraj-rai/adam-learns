<Hook emoji="🎯">
Laser shows at festivals and cricket stadiums use fast-moving mirrors to paint pictures in the sky. Engineers use mirrors to send laser beams around corners, along optical benches and even to the Moon and back! Can you plan a laser's path well enough to light every target, using as few mirrors as possible?
</Hook>

<ConceptCard title="Your toolkit" emoji="🧰">
- **∠i = ∠r**, measured from the normal.
- A mirror at **45°** turns a beam through **90°**.
- A **\\** mirror turns a rightward beam **down**; a **/** mirror turns a rightward beam **up**.
- A beam travels in a **straight line** until it hits something, and passes straight through a target.
- Plan the whole path **before** placing mirrors: work backwards from the target!
</ConceptCard>

<RealLife title="Lasers to the Moon">
Apollo astronauts left special mirrors on the Moon. Scientists still fire lasers at them and time the reflections, which measures the Earth–Moon distance to within a few centimetres!
</RealLife>

<CbseCorner source="Class 7 Curiosity Ch 11 and Class 8 Curiosity Ch 10">
Exam questions often ask you to **draw the path of a ray** reflected by one or two plane mirrors. Always draw the normal first, measure ∠i, and draw ∠r equal to it on the other side.
</CbseCorner>
