<Hook emoji="🎭">
In **tholu bommalata**, the leather shadow-puppet theatre of Andhra Pradesh, and **togalu gombeyaata** in Karnataka, puppeteers make giant heroes and demons dance on a cloth screen. As a puppet moves towards the lamp, its shadow grows huge! Why?
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A puppet moves CLOSER to the lamp. What happens to its shadow on the screen?"
  options={["It gets bigger", "It gets smaller", "It stays the same", "It disappears"]}
  answer={0}
>
Light spreads out from the lamp in **straight lines**. Close to the lamp, the puppet blocks a much wider cone of light, so the shadow grows.
</PredictFirst>

<ConceptCard title="Light travels in straight lines" emoji="➡️">
This is called <KeyTerm def="The property of light of travelling in straight lines.">rectilinear propagation</KeyTerm>. Because light can't bend around an opaque object, a dark region (a **shadow**) forms behind it. A shadow is always dark, whatever the colour of the object.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="shadow-theatre" />

<ConceptCard title="The pinhole camera" emoji="📷">
A box with a tiny hole at one end and a screen at the other makes an **upside-down** image. Rays from the top of the object pass straight through the hole to the bottom of the screen, and rays from the bottom land at the top. More proof that light travels in straight lines!
</ConceptCard>

<DidYouKnow>
During a solar eclipse, the gaps between leaves act like thousands of pinhole cameras, making tiny crescent-shaped images of the Sun on the ground. Never look at the Sun directly, even during an eclipse!
</DidYouKnow>
