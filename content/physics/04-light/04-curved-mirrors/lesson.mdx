<ConceptCard title="Important points" emoji="📍">
- **Pole (P)**: the centre of the mirror's surface.
- **Principal focus (F)**: where rays parallel to the axis meet after reflection (concave), or appear to come from (convex).
- **Centre of curvature (C)**: the centre of the sphere the mirror is part of. C is twice as far as F.
- **Real image**: light really meets there, so it can be caught on a screen. **Virtual image**: light only appears to come from there.
</ConceptCard>

<StepReveal>
<div>
**Concave mirror, object far away (beyond C):** real, inverted, diminished image between F and C.
</div>
<div>
**Object at C:** real, inverted, same size image at C.
</div>
<div>
**Object between F and C:** real, inverted, magnified image beyond C.
</div>
<div>
**Object at F:** reflected rays come out parallel, so there is no image. This is how torches make a beam!
</div>
<div>
**Object between P and F:** virtual, upright, magnified image behind the mirror. This is how shaving and make-up mirrors work.
</div>
</StepReveal>

<RealLife title="Concave mirrors at work">
Torch and headlight reflectors, dentists' mirrors, and **solar cookers**: a large concave mirror concentrates sunlight onto a pot at its focus. The Brahma Kumaris solar kitchen near Mount Abu, Rajasthan, uses rows of concave dishes to cook meals for thousands of people.
</RealLife>

<CbseCorner source="Class 8 Curiosity Ch 10">
- **Spherical mirrors**: concave and convex. Pole, focus, centre of curvature.
- A concave mirror can form real or virtual images; a convex mirror always forms virtual, erect, diminished images.
- Uses: concave (torches, headlights, dentists, solar cookers); convex (rear-view mirrors).
</CbseCorner>
