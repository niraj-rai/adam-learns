<Hook emoji="👂">
Your ear can detect a pressure change smaller than a billionth of normal air pressure, and tell apart thousands of different pitches. It does this with a flap of skin, a membrane thinner than paper, the three smallest bones in your body, and a snail-shaped tube full of liquid.
</Hook>

<ConceptCard title="Protect your hearing" emoji="🛡️">
Loudness is measured in <KeyTerm def="A unit for sound level. Every 10 dB increase means about 10 times the sound intensity.">decibels (dB)</KeyTerm>. Normal conversation is about 60 dB; heavy traffic 85 dB; firecrackers and loudspeakers can exceed 120 dB. Long exposure above about 85 dB damages the hair cells in the cochlea, and **they never grow back**.
- Keep earphones at no more than about 60% volume, for no more than about 60 minutes at a time.
- Use ear protection near very loud sounds.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="If loud music doesn't hurt, it can't damage your ears.">
Hearing damage from long exposure to moderately loud sound is painless and gradual. A ringing in the ears (tinnitus) after a concert is a warning sign that hair cells have been stressed.
</MythFact>
