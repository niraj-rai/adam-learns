<Hook emoji="🕌">
In the Gol Gumbaz in Vijayapura, Karnataka, one of the largest domes in the world, a whisper against the wall can be heard clearly on the opposite side of the gallery, and a single clap echoes several times. Sound **reflects** off hard surfaces, just like light off a mirror.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="Why don't you hear a separate echo when you clap in your bedroom?"
  options={["Sound doesn't reflect indoors", "Bedrooms absorb all sound", "You do, but it's too quiet", "The walls are too close, so the reflection returns too quickly"]}
  answer={3}

Your brain holds each sound for about **0.1 s**. A reflection from nearby walls returns sooner than that and blends with the original sound.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Echoes" />

<ConceptCard title="What is an echo?" emoji="⛰️">
An <KeyTerm def="A reflected sound heard separately from the original sound, at least 0.1 s later.">echo</KeyTerm> is a reflected sound heard separately from the original. Sound reflects best from large, hard surfaces: cliffs, buildings, bare walls.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="The 17.2 m rule" emoji="📏">
To be heard separately, the echo must return at least 0.1 s after the sound. In 0.1 s sound travels 344 × 0.1 = 34.4 m in air. That is there **and back**, so the wall must be at least **17.2 m** away.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="d = \frac{v \times t}{2}" caption="d = distance to the reflector, v = speed of sound, t = time for the echo to return" />

<Lab id="echo-canyon" />

<ConceptCard title="Reverberation" emoji="🔁">
In a big hall, sound reflects many times, and the reflections overlap, so each sound seems to last longer. This is <KeyTerm def="The persistence of sound in a space due to many overlapping reflections, making sounds seem to linger.">reverberation</KeyTerm>. A little makes music sound rich; too much makes speech blurry. Auditoriums use curtains, carpets, soft seats and sound-absorbing panels to control it, and curved ceilings to reflect sound towards the audience.
</ConceptCard>
