<Stage kind="wonder" title="Why is a flute higher than a tabla?" />

<Hook emoji="🎼">
A bansuri (bamboo flute) sings high notes; the bayan of a tabla booms low. A whisper and a shout of the same word differ in something else entirely. Every sound can be described by a few numbers: how big its vibrations are, how many happen each second, and how far apart the compressions are.
</Hook>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Describing a wave" />

<ConceptCard title="The key quantities" emoji="📏">
- <KeyTerm def="The maximum disturbance of the medium from its resting position; for sound, the size of the pressure change. Bigger amplitude = louder.">Amplitude</KeyTerm>: the size of the vibration. It decides **loudness**.
- <KeyTerm def="The number of complete vibrations (waves) per second, measured in hertz (Hz).">Frequency (f)</KeyTerm>: vibrations per second, in hertz (Hz). It decides **pitch**.
- **Time period (T)**: the time for one vibration. T = 1 ÷ f.
- <KeyTerm def="The distance between two neighbouring compressions (or rarefactions), or crests, of a wave.">Wavelength (λ)</KeyTerm>: the distance from one compression to the next.
</ConceptCard>

<Formula tex="v = f \lambda" caption="v = wave speed (m/s), f = frequency (Hz), λ = wavelength (m)" />

<ConceptCard title="Speed depends on the medium" emoji="🧱">
Sound travels about **344 m/s** in air at 22 °C, about **1500 m/s** in water and nearly **6000 m/s** in steel. The particles are closer and more tightly linked in liquids and solids, so they pass the vibration on faster. Sound is also a little faster in warm air than in cold air.
</ConceptCard>

<MythFact myth="Louder sounds travel faster.">
Loudness (amplitude) and pitch (frequency) don't change the speed. If they did, a band playing far away would sound jumbled, with high and loud notes arriving first!
</MythFact>
