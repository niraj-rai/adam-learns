<Stage kind="wonder" title="How does a sound reach you?" />

<Hook emoji="🥁">
When a drum is hit at the other end of a hall, you hear it almost instantly, yet no wind blows from the drum to you. Something travels across the room, but the air itself stays put. That "something" is a **wave**: a travelling pattern that carries energy.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A candle flame is placed in front of a loudspeaker playing a loud, low note. What does the flame do?"
  options={["Gets blown away from the speaker", "Nothing", "Flickers back and forth towards and away from the speaker", "Moves up and down"]}
  answer={2}
>
The air in front of the speaker is pushed and pulled back and forth **along** the direction of the sound. The flame jiggles the same way, but it isn't blown away.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="Longitudinal waves" />

<ConceptCard title="Compressions and rarefactions" emoji="🔊">
A vibrating object pushes the air particles next to it, which push the next ones, and so on. This makes a pattern of
- <KeyTerm def="A region of a longitudinal wave where the particles are crowded together, so the pressure is high.">compressions</KeyTerm> (particles squeezed together, high pressure) and
- <KeyTerm def="A region of a longitudinal wave where the particles are spread apart, so the pressure is low.">rarefactions</KeyTerm> (particles spread out, low pressure)

moving away from the source. Because the particles vibrate **parallel** to the direction the wave travels, sound is a <KeyTerm def="A wave in which the particles of the medium vibrate back and forth in the same direction as the wave travels.">longitudinal wave</KeyTerm>.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="longitudinal-wave" />

<MythFact myth="In space battles, you'd hear the explosions.">
Space is almost a perfect vacuum, with no particles to pass the vibrations along. Sound needs a **medium**: a solid, liquid or gas. Astronauts talk by radio, which uses electromagnetic waves that can cross a vacuum.
</MythFact>

<TryAtHome
  title="Slinky waves"
  materials={["a slinky spring (or a long soft spring)", "a smooth floor or table", "a friend"]}
  safety="Don't overstretch the slinky or let it snap back towards anyone's face."
>
Stretch the slinky along the floor between you and a friend. Push one end forwards and back sharply: watch a compression travel along. Then shake it sideways to make a transverse wave. Tie a ribbon to one coil: does the ribbon travel along the slinky?
</TryAtHome>
