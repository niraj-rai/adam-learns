<Hook emoji="🏃">
Two friends of the same mass climb the same stairs. One strolls up in 30 seconds; the other sprints up in 10. They do exactly the **same work**, so what's different? The sprinter does it three times as **fast**: they are more powerful.
</Hook>

Power is energy per second. 60 W means 60 joules every second, so in the same time the 60 W bulb uses more than six times as much energy.

<Stage kind="explain" title="Power" />

<Formula tex="P = \frac{W}{t}" caption="P = power (W), W = work done or energy transferred (J), t = time (s)" />

<ConceptCard title="Units of power" emoji="⚡">
- 1 <KeyTerm def="The SI unit of power: 1 watt = 1 joule per second.">watt (W)</KeyTerm> = 1 J/s.
- 1 kilowatt (kW) = 1000 W.
- An old unit, 1 horsepower ≈ 746 W, is still used for engines.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="The unit on your bill" emoji="🧾">
Electricity boards charge for energy, not power. They use a bigger energy unit, the <KeyTerm def="The energy used by a 1 kW appliance running for 1 hour. 1 kWh = 3.6 million joules = 1 'unit' of electricity.">kilowatt-hour (kWh)</KeyTerm>:

energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h)

A 1.5 kW air conditioner running for 4 hours uses 6 kWh, or 6 **units**.
</ConceptCard>

<Stage kind="try" title="Power Up" />

<Lab id="power-stairs" />

<MythFact myth="A more powerful machine always does more work.">
Power is about **how fast** work is done, not how much. A powerful motor finishes the same job sooner; a weaker one takes longer but can do the same total work.
</MythFact>

<RealLife>
The star ratings from India's Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) on fridges, ACs and fans show how efficiently they use energy. A 5-star appliance does the same job with less electrical energy, saving money and reducing the coal burnt in power stations.
</RealLife>
