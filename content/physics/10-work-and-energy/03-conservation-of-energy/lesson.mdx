<Stage kind="wonder" title="Where does the energy go?" />

<Hook emoji="🏀">
Drop a ball. It bounces back up, but never quite as high. The next bounce is lower again, until it rolls to a stop. Has its energy vanished? One of the most important laws in all of science says **no**: energy can never be created or destroyed.
</Hook>

<PredictFirst
  question="A pendulum swings for a long time and then stops. What happened to its energy?"
  options={["It was destroyed", "It was used up by gravity", "It turned into heat and sound in the air and the string", "It is still in the pendulum as potential energy"]}
  answer={2}
>
Air resistance and friction at the pivot transfer a little energy each swing to the **surroundings** as heat and sound. The total amount of energy is unchanged; it's just spread out.
</PredictFirst>

<Stage kind="explain" title="The law of conservation of energy" />

<ConceptCard title="Energy is conserved" emoji="🔒">
Energy can be **transferred** from one object to another and **transformed** from one form to another, but it can never be created or destroyed. The total energy of an isolated system stays the same.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptCard title="Falling objects" emoji="🥭">
With no air resistance, the <KeyTerm def="The sum of an object's kinetic and potential energy.">mechanical energy</KeyTerm> (KE + PE) stays constant as something falls. The potential energy lost equals the kinetic energy gained:

mgh = ½mv², so **v = √(2gh)**. The mass cancels out, so every object dropped from the same height lands at the same speed.
</ConceptCard>

<Lab id="energy-skate-park" />

<ConceptCard title="Wasted energy" emoji="🔥">
In real machines some energy always ends up heating the surroundings (friction, air resistance, sound). We say it is <KeyTerm def="Spread out into the surroundings, usually as heat, where it is hard to use again.">dissipated</KeyTerm>. It isn't destroyed, but it is no longer useful. Lubricating moving parts and streamlining vehicles reduce this waste.
</ConceptCard>
